## 1. Official NFL tiebreakers

**Division, two teams:** Head-to-head → division record → common games → conference record → strength of victory → strength of schedule → combined conference points ranking → combined league points ranking → net points in common games → net points overall → net touchdowns → coin toss.

**Division, 3+ teams:** Same order, using head-to-head winning percentage among tied teams.

**Wild card, two teams from different divisions:** Head-to-head → conference record → common games (minimum four) → strength of victory → strength of schedule → combined conference points ranking → combined league points ranking → conference net points → overall net points → net touchdowns → coin toss.

**Wild card, 3+ teams:** First eliminate all but the highest-ranked team from each division. Then apply head-to-head sweep → conference record → common games (minimum four) → remaining wild-card criteria.

Tiebreakers restart when teams are separated. Ties count as half a win and half a loss.

## 7. Published scheduling constraints

The NFL's published scheduling information establishes:

- 17 games across 18 weeks.
- One bye per team.
- Nine home/eight away games or vice versa.
- 272 regular-season games.

### Home/away streaks

The NFL has publicly described attempts to avoid:

- More than three consecutive home or road games.
- Consecutive cross-country trips.
- Teams crossing three time zones for Thursday games.
- Consecutive road games at the beginning or end of the season.

These are scheduling objectives, not universally enforced rules. Exceptions occur.

Source: [NFL schedule-making explanation](https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-schedule-from-500-000-possibilities-to-a-final-one-0ap1000000161953?utm_source=chatgpt.com).
