## The Owner's question

The flex derivation has been a headache. Deriving a formula out of drive-level data — slot wins, measured weights — plus the Owner's philosophy, which he has to input in English (the questionnaire, the rulings) and which then has to be translated into math, keeps producing friction: the center-to-tackle step-up, the fullback problem, the guard breaking what the ability layer gets right, valuing a new role that has no slot yet.

The Owner's question: is that confusion a bigger headache than just letting the engine realize, at play level, which players are affecting the plays — generating its own snap counts and stats, tracking who is valuable — so the engine generates the math itself, instead of the Owner having to input his philosophy in English and have it translated into a formula?

And: we would not be starting from zero. The drive engine, the matchup model, the slot values, the duty tables, all the derivation work — that knowledge informs a play-level build. The calibrated numbers transfer.

## Background (so this prompt stands alone — assume no prior context)

The engine is drive-level (top-down). The matchup model computes expected totals (yards, points, turnovers, sacks) from unit ratings. The drive generator distributes those totals across ~11.5 drives per team. The box score allocates player stats from the drive totals. There is no per-play state: no down, no distance, no field position. There is no per-player snap count — the engine does not know how many plays a player was on the field for, only what stats he was allocated. A flex price (the penalty for playing out of position) is currently derived from drive-level aggregates: slot wins (how many wins a slot adds) and measured slot values, plus the Owner's hand-priced 99 rules as a guide. The 99 are a guide, not a target.

The derivation's persistent headaches, all from trying to get the formula out of drive data plus English philosophy:

- **C>OT:** the ability layer prices a center moving to tackle at 6; the Owner says 12. The value ladder (guard) fixes it to 12, but the guard then breaks other rows.
- **The fullback:** the guard prices FB>RB at 18 (FB's slot weight is only 25, so the ladder is steep); the Owner says 2. His new principle is "socialism" — if a role has one good player and is unimportant, its flex values are reduced in both directions — but socialism has no measurement yet, because the engine does not track how often a fullback is actually on the field. The fact that "the fullback only plays a few plays" is knowledge the Owner supplied in English, not knowledge the engine has.
- **Valuing a new role:** a big nickel (SS2) or a DL5 blitzer has no slot in the sim, so there is no measured value for it. The Owner defines the requirements; the engine has to value the role. That is the hard part.
- **The gate** (a value-ladder condition) was built, reached 99/99, and then removed by the Owner. The derivation is now ability-only at 98/99, C>OT the lone miss — and the Owner says C>OT at 6 is fine unless centers constantly flex out in the sim.

There is a middle path worth weighing: a play-level VIEW on top of the drives. The drive outcome stays authoritative, and a play sequence is generated inside each drive that adds up to it (down, distance, field position, who is on the field, who touched it). From that you read off participation, contribution, EPA. The engine "sees" those things because it produced the plays. But the plays are generated, not simulated — the drive determines the plays, not the other way around — and the personnel model (who is on the field for each play) and the attribution model (who got the ball, who made the stop) still have to be built. The question is whether that actually solves the problem or just moves the translation into those models.

A full play-level REBUILD is the other shape: the plays determine the drive, bottom-up. That would be more causally consistent (a player's EPA and the team's wins come from the same simulation), but it bets the game on a new play model being good enough to reproduce the drive totals, and it means re-measuring the calibrated numbers.

The Owner's direction that still binds (from his Q&A, at `_agents/muse/deliveries/questionnaire-flex-derivation-QA-2026-11-10.md`): the 99 are a guide, not a target; beautiful and portable wins; inputs are OVR + position only; no gates, no floor, no named exceptions; dual positions free; gravity is now, inside the value ideally; no Owner set weights for other sports; ±5 points of flex error is acceptable; socialism for one-good-player unimportant roles; emergence of requirements is impossible (the Owner defines them, the engine prices who can flex in); snap share cannot be intuited by a formula and is not consistent across sports.

## The builders' answers so far (read before answering)

### Z.ai's answer — landed (the "Plays Ledger")

**File:** `_agents/vl/deliveries/z-play-by-play-analysis/_agents/zai/deliveries/ZAI-PLAY-LEVEL-ANSWER-2026-11-11.md` on `origin/council/vl`, commit `9ede94bf`. **MUSE's review:** `inbox/20261111-0008-muse-P-341-zai-play-level-review.md` (VERDICT: improved) — read both the answer and the review; the review found the design strong but the worked numbers in Appendix A not reproducing, and two rows with the wrong tier.

Z.ai's answer in brief: the engine should see play level, but **not by becoming a play-level simulator**. His shape is the **Plays Ledger**: the drive engine stays authoritative physics, and inside each drive the engine *stages* the plays that add up to the drive's outcome — down, distance, field position, personnel, who touched it, who blocked whom. Plays as an audit trail, not a second physics. His five answers: (1) two of the four headaches dissolve (FB/socialism and pricing roles with no slot); C>OT survives but is split into ability vs value; the translation problem is upgraded from fitting constants to authoring mechanisms with measurable consequences. (2) We are not starting from zero — roughly 70% of the machinery transfers. (3) The middle path (the Ledger) is the correct shape, not a compromise — and the box score is the existence proof (it already generates play-shaped facts from drive totals and they sum exactly). (4) The path is five shippable steps, starting with a snap ledger that needs no play sequencer at all. (5) The engine generates the math via two loops that need each other: the Ledger *measures* (exposure, contribution, gravity) and the lab *experiments* (perturb a roster, re-simulate, count wins). A flex price falls out as `max(measured ability, exposure × measured guard)` — no gates, no floor, no named exceptions. His load-bearing new idea is the **exposure-weighted guard = socialism's arithmetic**: the guard is scaled by the source role's measured exposure (snap share), so FB>RB collapses from 18 to ~2 without a gate, while C>OT (exposure ~1) keeps the full guard at 12.

**What MUSE's review found (so you can check it yourself):** the engine facts he cites all verify; the guard arithmetic is correct; but the Appendix A ability numbers are wrong for most rows (recomputed from ZAI-D1 with the three score changes — e.g. FB>TE ability is 4.74, not "~1–2"; OLB>FS ability is 11.74, not "~8–9"), and two rows flip on corrected numbers (FB>TE becomes an exact match with the Owner at tier 6, not "one tier low"; OLB>FS becomes an exact match at tier 12, not a miss). The "10-line arithmetic check" he mentions is not in the delivery, so the numbers do not reproduce as filed. Correcting the numbers makes his design look *better*, not worse — but the demonstration cannot be cited as filed. The review lists the required corrections.

### The interactions — what the council should weigh in on

These are the tensions between Z.ai's answer, Claude's earlier work, and the question itself. The council's full opinion is wanted on each:

1. **Is the Plays Ledger a third shape, or the play-view with a better name?** MUSE proposed a "play-level view" (drives authoritative, plays generated inside them) before Z.ai answered. Z.ai's Ledger is the same shape with a name, the box-score existence proof, and the exposure-weighted guard attached. Is there a real difference — and if not, does the Ledger add anything beyond the name and the guard?
2. **Does the exposure-weighted guard survive the corrected numbers?** MUSE's review re-ran Appendix A and found FB>TE and OLB>FS become exact matches on corrected numbers. Verify that independently (the ZAI-D1 script is on council/glyph). Does the guard still fix the FB family without breaking anything else, once the ability numbers are right? And is the FB tier stable — Z.ai's exposure band (0.15–0.25) straddles the tier cut at 4.0, so the tier flips inside his own band.
3. **Does the Ledger solve the English-philosophy problem, or move it?** Z.ai says the translation is upgraded (philosophy becomes mechanisms with measurable consequences), not eliminated. The personnel model (who lines up where) and the attribution model (who blocked whom) are new authoring surfaces. Is authoring "who lines up where" genuinely the Owner's home turf (football facts he can state), or is it the same translation problem in a new place? Where exactly does philosophy still enter, and is that acceptable?
4. **Is Step 1 (snap ledger, no sequencer) actually shippable and sufficient?** Z.ai's path starts with per-slot snap counts from the existing drive→boxscore allocation, with no play sequencer. Does that really end the FB problem on its own — and can the council verify the snap shares against nflverse (the source `dl5-snaps.py` already ingests) before anything else is built?
5. **The two loops (Ledger measures, Lab experiments) — is that the right split?** Z.ai says a staged play cannot discover what a different lineup would have done; only the lab's perturb-and-resimulate can. Is keeping the observational layer and the experimental layer separate the right call, or should they be one instrument? What does the split cost (and what does merging them cost)?
6. **What would Claude's answer have to say to change this?** Claude's flexunify already showed a measured guard "kills the center exploit outright" with zero under-priced rows — but at 84/97 fit. Z.ai's Ledger keeps the drive engine and adds exposure. If Claude's pending answer argues for a full rebuild instead, what evidence would settle it — and what would the council need to see to prefer one over the other?
