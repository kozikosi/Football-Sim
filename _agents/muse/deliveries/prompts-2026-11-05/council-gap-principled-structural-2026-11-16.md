# Council prompt — a principled formula AND the structural side of the 4-3/3-4 gap (FOR: P-333)

**What this is.** The Owner (KG) rejected every re-pricing option for closing the 4-3/3-4 formation gap, because they all violate the principles that determine the rest of the table. His words: "they all violate principels that determine the rest of the table." This round tries the two directions he chose instead. Your job is to propose and measure — do not pick one, and do not implement anything. KG picks.

**The gap, as measured.** A "stand-in" is a player flexed into a position he does not normally play, at a penalty. The penalty is a number — 0, 2, 6, or 12 — multiplied by the destination position's value. The 4-3 roster loses 2500 in 3-4 (three stand-ins: DT2 from OLB at 6, SAM from TE at 6, TE3 from FB at 2). The 3-4 roster loses 1800 in 4-3 (one stand-in: DL5 from ILB at 12). Gap = 2500 − 1800 = 700 table units / about 720 measured = 0.09 wins a season. (FB to TE is 2; the DIME trio — TE, ILB, and OLB to the DIME slot — is 12.)

**The principles — read this carefully, it is the whole constraint.** The 99-rule flex table's specific prices are guidelines (the engine need not conform to every price). But the PRINCIPLES behind the table are what determine those prices, and an ad hoc change to one price breaks the logic that sets the others. The principles include: the destination-weight and athletic-distance logic that sets the base price for each pair; the DIME trio (TE/ILB/OLB to DIME is expensive at 12 because DIME is coverage-heavy and those players are poor in coverage); the FB exception (DT/OT to FB at 0, FB to TE at 2); and the cross-side floor (wide receivers to cornerback, free safety, strong safety, nickel, and dime at 6, because crossing sides is expensive). KG likes the 0/2/6/12 tier structure and does not want a new tier (a penalty of 4 adds user-facing complexity), though he can be convinced to abandon the structure. A proposal that changes one price just to close the gap, without a principled reason, will be rejected — that is what happened to the last round.

**What the last round produced (so you do not repeat it).** Inkling, Ernie, Beam, VL, and Glyph filed re-pricing options. The verified results: Ernie's one-rule SS to ILB 6→4 (gap 100) and Glyph's one-rule TE to OLB 6→0 (gap 100) both use a single lever but change a price without a principled reason. Inkling's DIME trio 12→6 (gap 400, already approved as Option B) and 12→2 (gap 200) change the DIME trio. Glyph's and Inkling's two-rule zeros (gap 0) unwind the FB exception. Beam's ILB to DL5 12→6 + TE to SAM 6→12 WIDENS the gap to 1600 (sign error, refuted). Ernie also found a second, larger gap: on real rosters, 28 teams are better in 4-3 and only 3 in 3-4, mean bias +1144 units (0.145 wins), and no re-pricing closes it below ~947 — because real rosters are 4-3-shaped bodies. The re-pricing route is closed. Do not file another re-pricing option.

## Part A — A principled formula

Find a formula (or a principled mechanism) that derives the flex prices from the same logic that determines the table AND closes the gap, without breaking the DIME trio, the FB exception, or the cross-side floor — or, if it must change one of those, justify why the principle allows it.

What exists already (do not repeat without improving): Ernie's ladder (destination weight + athletic distance, best fit 50/99, prices DIME at 4–5 vs 12, breaks the FB exception, flexing doubles 145→245–413, gap flips sign); flexderive (87/99 exact, gap effect unmeasured); Inkling's distance formula (gap 2773→1014 on one direction only, QB to FS gives 6 vs the pin 12); Beam's distance-times-leverage formula (keeps DIME at 12 via a coverage override, FB to TE at 2, but never demonstrated closing the gap). For each candidate you propose, measure and report: the fit to the 99 rules (exact / off by one tier / hard misses), the new gap (table and measured), which of the DIME trio / FB exception / cross-side floor it keeps or changes (and the principled reason), and the effect on flexing frequency and emergency fills. If no formula can do all of this, say so with the measurement — a negative result is a result.

## Part B — The structural side (the real-roster bias)

Investigate Ernie's real-roster bias and propose a fix on the pool/slot side, not the flex table. The finding (verified): on the 32 real rosters, 28 teams are better in 4-3 and 3 in 3-4 (the Cardinals are exactly tied), mean signed bias +1144 units (0.145 wins), and the best re-pricing only reaches ~947. The bias is driven by pool shape — real rosters are 4-3-shaped, so 3-4 has fewer natural bodies for its two outside linebacker spots and its second inside linebacker, which is rated off DT2 (weight 300). Your job: identify exactly which slot-table or pool-shape facts drive the bias, and propose a fix on that side (for example: whether 3-4's second inside linebacker should be rated off DT2 at all, or a change to the 3-4 spot table). Measure the effect of each proposal on the real bias (the 28-3 split and the mean signed bias) and on the synthetic gap. This is an Owner/Architect decision about the spot table, so propose and measure only — do not change any slot values. If the bias cannot be fixed on the pool/slot side without a larger change, say what that larger change would be.

## Filing

Write your proposal in your `inbox/` as a filing labeled FOR: P-333, and push it. Push after every finished file. State which part you are answering (A, B, or both). KG picks; no implementation until he does. If you find a flaw in another seat's proposal, file it as your own measurement, not as a correction to someone else's file.

## Innovation

Think around the issue, not just inside it. Brainstorm, improve, and create new ideas — the prompt tells you what to propose, not how to think. If you see a mechanism nobody has named yet — a different derivation, a different structural lever — name it and measure it. A principled mechanism that nobody has tried is worth more than another re-pricing.
