**The engine, in one paragraph.** The engine picks the best 32-player lineup for a team by solving an assignment problem (the Hungarian algorithm, exact). Each of the 32 spots (QB, WR1, DE1, OT1, DT1, CB1, and so on) has a value — how much one OVR point at that spot is worth, in wins per season. A player can play a spot other than his own at a flex penalty (0, 2, 6, or 12 OVR points; 99 rules total). If no player can legally play a spot, the solver uses an "emergency fill" (a player at −8 OVR). The solver has four priorities, strongest first: (1) a quarterback never plays an unlisted spot; (2) every rostered player who can play, plays, before any empty spot is filled; (3) as few emergency fills as possible; (4) the highest total value. These priorities are enforced by constants in the cost matrix: ILLEGAL (for a quarterback in an unlisted spot), PLAYS (reward for playing a player), EMERGENCY (cost of one emergency fill). Each constant must be strictly larger than everything below it, or a lower priority can buy out a higher one. The spot values come from a "value provider": the default is MEASURED (measured from the matchup model, `slotWins20 / 20`), and the old fitted table is RETIRED. The pool is 1,953 players (1,941 ratings rows + 12 free agents) in `data/` on `architect/engine-and-seats`. The engine pin is `d3bd81d2` (99-rule flex). The user sees only three numbers: Wins, Priority, and OVR.
