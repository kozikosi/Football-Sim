# QUEUE.md — the ranked work list (the only queue)

**NOBODY IS ASSIGNED TO ANYTHING.** Agents are often unavailable (OpenChamber was down for a day; DS is rarely on). Whoever opens a chat runs `node tools/council.mjs next` (v9, from `origin/council/muse:tools/council.mjs`) and takes the top line it prints for them. There is no claiming: either you do the work (or the review) and push it, or you leave it for someone else. Nothing waits for a particular seat, ever.

**The live queue is on `origin/council/muse`.** `council/base`'s queue file is a stale ghost (last moved 2026-11-02); do not take work from it.

**The chain for every item (council work = ideas, exact snippets, measurements, mock-ups; only the Architect edits the product):**
1. **WORK** by the first seat that takes it: `inbox/<UTC>-<seat>-<ID>-<slug>.md` (format: `inbox/README.md`).
2. **REVIEW** by three *different* seats who come later: each reads the work, checks it against the repo, and either confirms or **improves** it (`inbox/<UTC>-<seat>-<ID>-review.md`, `REVIEW-OF:`, `VERDICT: ok | improved | rejected`, improved text included). A rejection sends the item back to OPEN. More than three reviews are fine; no seat reviews the same item twice.
3. After three confirmations the item is **REVIEWED ✓✓✓**: it is ready for the Architect to build. Its dependants move up one tier (shown with ↑). Further reviews by new seats are still welcome.
4. After the Architect builds it, a test order (`_agents/TEST-ORDERS/`) is run by any seat that supplied nothing for that build; three PASS results mark the build item reviewed. The item then moves to the RETIRED section below as BUILT.
A review you can do beats new work in the same tier. High Councillors (GPT, Z.ai) follow the same chain; the Owner's tiers are not theirs to change. An item overtaken by later work or Owner wording moves to the RETIRED section as SUPERSEDED (names what superseded it). Terminal states are moved by the Owner, the Architect, or MUSE (custodian, standing permission — Owner 2026-11-08 to move items to BUILT; Owner 2026-11-09, MUSE owns council infrastructure and keeps the queue honest). **The Owner does not read or sweep this file manually, and no per-wave Owner note is required for queue hygiene** (Owner 2026-11-09). A sweep cites (a) the standing permission and (b) the evidence for each item. The Nov 5 retire citation was corrected: `_agents/RETIRES-CITATION-CORRECTION-2026-11-09.md`.

**Tiers:** P0 = the Owner's current V23 requests · P1 = Owner-stated, earlier lists (21 Oct work order and addendum) · P2 = enablers, checks and improvements (ours and the council's, not yet finalised) · P3 = ideas. Terminal states (RETIRED section below): SUPERSEDED = overtaken by later work/wording · BUILT = the Architect shipped it. Only the Owner or the Architect re-ranks or retires; seats propose with a `PRIORITY+: <item> | <tier> | <why>` line in their bulletin entry (the MUSE seat also holds standing Owner permission to add proposals directly, and — since 2026-11-08 — to move items to BUILT). Later Owner wording beats earlier (e.g. 24 Oct homepage rules replace the 23 Oct tagline text).

**Definition — skilled (Owner, locked 2026-11-05; supersedes "top 30% by draft skill" and retires the term "top30"):** a skilled drafter selects within the top 30 players ranked by priority (the draft value metric — Wins/Priority, not raw OVR) in every draft round. This defines a class, not a single pick: it covers everyone from the drafter who always takes #1 by priority to the one who always takes #30, with the average in the middle. The calibration targets apply to the AVERAGE skilled drafter — the middle of the range. The Lab's `skilled` policy (formerly `top30`: picks uniformly at random among the top 30 by Wins) measures that average. Use "skilled" everywhere; do not say "top30".
