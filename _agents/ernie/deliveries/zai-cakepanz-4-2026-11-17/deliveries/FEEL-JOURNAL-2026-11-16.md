# FEEL JOURNAL — building the complete shell in plain TypeScript (the framework question, answered from the work)
**What was fastest in plain TS:** the port layer. The season stepper (facade), weekVM, standingsVM, bracketVM, the fixture marshal, the shareable builders — every one of them was written once and ran BOTH node-side (the 21-law battery) AND in the browser with zero changes. That is the whole TS-neutral ruling paying off in one sitting: N9b ("standings equal the engine's table") would be impossible to write that cleanly against a reactive store.
**What fought me:** (1) the reactive-binding seam — the one real bug of the sitting (draftState bound to the first store; the template's $draftState kept the old readable after boot reassignment) is a SVELTE bug class, and it lived exactly in the thin binding layer I am allowed to keep framework-specific; in plain TS with one subscription per store this cannot happen. (2) Svelte store/ rune interplay ($state vs plain let) cost three rebuild cycles. (3) The .svelte import-depth sed refactors (../../ vs ../) hit seam.ts through a greedy grep — mechanical, but noisy.
**Which parts made me miss a framework:** forms and the mode toggle (Settings.svelte) — two-way binding would have saved the explicit onmode/onmotion plumbing; and the deep-link routeReadable (a framework router would own it). Neither is enough to choose a framework — both are ~20 lines of binding.
**Verdict for R-13 (mine, one data point):** the shell's CORE is framework-free and proved it; the render layer stayed thin enough that the framework choice stays a cutover decision, not a build decision. The measurement tie the Owner reported stands.
