# 1 · Should 120,000 keys be the house standard, or 12,000 with 120,000 reserved for the headline?

**120,000 by default, but not for the reason the noise lesson suggests.** The lesson everyone drew is "12,000 was noisy, 120,000 fixed it". That is true and it is the shallow reading. The deeper fact is that **the paired design makes 120,000 nearly free**: a paired run's cost is two engine runs per key, and my own 12,000-key adjudication ran in 24 s, so 120,000 is ~4 minutes. Four minutes is not a reason to accept a standard error 3.2× wider than necessary. What 12,000 *is* good for is the inner loop — a seat iterating on a script wants 20-second feedback — so the honest rule is not "12,000 default, 120,000 for headlines", which keeps the noisy number in circulation and relies on seats remembering to re-run. It is **"report at 120,000; iterate at whatever you like"**. The failure mode this round actually had was not that someone ran 12,000 — it was that a 12,000 number was *reported* and then compared against a 120,000 number as if they were the same measurement. A house standard that says "the number you file is the 120,000 number; the number you debug with is never filed" removes that. One caveat worth writing down: the headline is not always the biggest number. When the question is *existence* (does a fault plant die, does a law fire), 1,000 keys is plenty and 120,000 is waste. The standard should be **120,000 for any rate or mean that will be quoted, and any N for a pass/fail**, with the N stated in the filing either way.

# 2 · Are council tools the right shape, or do they need a different door than filings?

**Different door, and the door is a queue row, not a filing.** The tell is in this round's own experience: three seats independently wrote a bucket-bar, which is three times the work and one tool's worth of value, and the prompt then had to spend a Part adjudicating between them. That is not a council failure — it is what happens when a tool is proposed the same way an idea is proposed. An idea wants three variants because the variants are cheap and the comparison is the product. A tool wants one implementation and three *reviews*, because the review is the product. So: **a tool should be proposed as a queue row whose deliverable is the tool plus a review by a seat who did not write it**, exactly the shape the Architect's build already has. The filing stays for the *decision* (why this tool, what it must print, what its falsifier is), but the file itself belongs in `tools/` under the Architect or MUSE, landed once, with the three seats' contributions becoming the review chain rather than three parallel implementations. Two guardrails that this round shows are needed: **every tool ships with a check that can fail on the tool itself** (my bucket-bar's 2016–2022 falsifier is the model — it prints 271%/−171% rather than a pinned 100%, which is what proves it is measuring), and **no tool lands without a stated N and a stated runtime**, because "it ran on my machine" is the machine-only-battery failure with extra steps. One thing I would *not* change: the pin-watch idea (Inkling's) should stay a council tool, not a product tool — it guards the council's own hygiene, and it is cheap enough (a stale pin line vs the tree's own bytes) that an afternoon is the right budget.
