**Position in one sentence (INFERRED, design):** form is a **rolling four-game z-score of a player's measured performance against his own slot's baseline**, mapped to a ±6 OVR band, **reset at the season boundary**, and displayed as the Owner's binary arrow — nothing else reaches the screen.

## The mechanism, inside the Owner's answers

- **What form is:** a rolling window (last 4 games, newer weighted higher) of the player's game grade — the engine already measures per-player production in the box score, so form is built from what the engine measures, per the constraint. `form_z = (recent − baseline) / sd`, where the baseline is the player's OWN season-to-date mean (so a star regressing to greatness shows cold even while elite in absolute terms — which is what "form" means to a fan).
- **How it moves OVR by ~5–6 (verbatim: "around 5-6 during the season"):** `ovr_display = ovr + round(6 · clamp(form_z / 2.5, −1, 1))` — full saturation at z=±2.5. The rounding keeps integers (style law). The ±6 band is a parameter the Owner can re-dial; the *shape* (linear z-clamp) is the proposal.
- **Reset each season (the standing 2026-11-13 framing):** yes — form is a within-season narrative device; carry-over would smuggle a second, hidden ratings system across drafts. Reset is also the honest reading of "during the course of the season" in the displayed-vs-underlying ruling.
- **The arrow is binary (verbatim: "a simple GREEN arrow… a simple RED arrow… NO emojis. NO 'HOT' or 'cold' text labels"):** the arrow fires at |z| ≥ 1.0, neutral shows nothing. Graded intensity is deliberately NOT proposed — the Owner chose simple twice, and a second "how hot" scale would be a second number the consistency ruling must then carry.
- **The marker (sketch, not build):** the arrow sits as a superscript glyph immediately after the OVR integer on every surface that shows OVR (player card, depth chart, draft board popover), in the team palette's "good"/"bad" tokens — the same palette discipline the w18 comparison numbers use. No tooltip on hover in v1; the Guide carries one explanatory sentence ("The arrow marks the last four games — it moves the OVR you see by up to six points.").

## Receipt

- The formula is one pure function of measured box-score data — testable the day the sim emits per-player game grades into the staging (the P-344 participation cut names which file). Named receipt: week-to-week OVR Δ distribution vs flat on a 2023–25 holdout; accept the ±6 band only if the *observed* z-spread of real players covers it (if real players' 4-game z-scores rarely reach ±2.5, the band saturates never and the arrow dies — shrink to the measured 90th percentile instead of hand-setting 2.5).
- Cheap first cut available now: the P-344 team-game file gives team-level form (rolling net points) — the same z-clamp shape at team level for a dry run. Not run this wave (the player-grade join is the load-bearing piece and it is not in the staging yet — named, not faked).

## Failure mode

- **The lottery board:** if form swung ±6 on the DRAFT BOARD, a cold elite player would look droppable — the arrow must appear on the board only as the glyph (no value change), because **displayed OVR to the user is static** under the displayed-vs-underlying ruling; the ±6 lives in the *underlying* effective OVR the sim consumes, and the arrow TELLS the user it exists without moving the printed integer. This is the exact split Ruling 13 draws — this filing is the worked example of it.
- **Noise-as-narrative:** a 4-game window on a low-touch position (OL) is mostly noise. Guard: minimum-opportunity gate (the arrow needs ≥ N measured events in the window; below N, no arrow) — same pattern as the D.2 steepness guard.

## The Owner-question it carries

Is the arrow **game-by-game** (updates every week, the fan's streak story) or **smoothed** (the 4-game window, steadier)? My default: 4-game window — three bad quarters of one game should not turn a 99 cold, and the Owner's "~5-6 during the season" phrasing reads as season-scale, not week-scale. Consequence of the other choice: arrows churn weekly and the user learns to ignore them.
