# Why the pass-class GLM moved sacks and drives on a whole game (Architect, 18 Nov 2026)

Answer to MUSE's request of 17 Nov (`glm-pass-class-2026-11-17.md`, `65673a00`). A REPORT: nothing here gates a build. VERIFIED = run this session; INFERRED = my reasoning, not tested.

## 1. Answer

The shift was **not** one thing and **not** a bias in the GLM.

1. **Sacks (−0.04 points per attempt, 7.043% to 7.002%) are the GLM being score- and clock-aware in a simulation whose score and clock mix is not the real one.** The pools node ignores score; the GLM does not. Reweighting the nodes' real disagreement to the sim's score-and-clock mix predicts −0.062 points; measured −0.050. Down-and-distance, field position and the pool's own cell predict almost nothing (−0.017, −0.022, +0.007). On the real 2023-25 states both nodes agree with the data (sack 7.047% pool, 7.026% GLM, 7.025% actual). It is **not** the 6.4% to 7.0% drift (section 3).
2. **Drives had a defect in my own class-then-tuple sampler.** A control arm (the same sampler fed the pool's own class mix) isolated it: the sampler read tuples across downs. Fixed in `02eebdf` (section 4).
3. **Found on the way: the pools node itself under-ends 4th-down drives** (section 6). Not fixed here; my proposal is in section 7.

## 2. Method (all VERIFIED)

`colors/tools/glm-shift.ts`, 40,000 games, three arms on the same keys: **A** pools node, **C** control (class-then-tuple sampler fed the pool's exact class mix, via the lab hook `runGame opts.classMix`), **G** the GLM. C−A is the sampler alone, G−C the GLM's probabilities alone. Every pass snap's state is read back from the event log (score difference rebuilt from the score events) and both nodes' exact class probabilities are evaluated at it, so rate(G sim) − rate(A sim) = RATE effect (nodes disagree at the same states) + MIX effect (different states visited) is exact. Sanity: each arm's realized class rates equal its own node's expected rates (A 7.029 vs 7.043, G 7.001 vs 7.002).

Real states: `python3 -I tools/mathlab/phase1/passstates.py OUT.csv 2023 2025` (59,073 pass plays). Run: `node --experimental-strip-types colors/tools/glm-shift.ts 40000 OUT.csv sack` (also `comp`).

## 3. Falsifier: is it the 6.4% to 7.0% sack-rate drift? No (VERIFIED)

`glm-bar.ts 40000`, sampler as fixed. The drift would appear when the pool and GLM come from different eras and vanish when they match:

| pools / GLM fit on | sacks | drives | points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023-25 / 2023-25 (product) | −1.05% (z −3.3) | −0.18% | −0.09% |
| 2016-22 / 2016-22 | −0.27% (z −0.8) | −0.33% | −1.12% |
| **2023-25 / 2016-22 (cross-era)** | **−8.99% (z −29)** | +0.30% | +0.06% |

The cross-era row is what drift looks like (−9%, matching 6.4 to 7.0). The product pair is an order of magnitude smaller. Drift killed.

## 4. Drives: the sampler defect (VERIFIED, fixed `02eebdf`)

Exact P(this pass snap ends the drive), at the pools sim's states: **4th down: pools node 47.5%, old sampler 23.7%, down-preserving sampler 63.5%; real records 65.5%.** On 3rd down the old sampler went the other way (+0.27 points). Cause: a tuple's next down is absolute, so a 3rd-down incompletion (next: 4th) filled in for a failed 4th-down try and the drive lived; the old hierarchy's level 1 merges 3rd and 4th, levels 2-3 drop the down. 88% of 4th-down incompletions and 72% of completions were read from level 1. Anchors: 4th-and-1 on the 3 (every outcome ends the drive) 0.541 → 0.989.

Fix: `Pools.recDown`, a down-preserving hierarchy built from the same records; no pack or save change; the default node is untouched. Tests: exact level totals, the anchors, and a 400-game guard (the planted old hierarchy gives 105 of 217 failed 4th-down passes ending the drive; the fix ≥ 90%). 409/409; tsc ratchet 89/89.

After the fix, paired 40k (per team-game):

| | C−A (sampler) | G−C (GLM) | G−A (both) |
|---|---|---|---|
| drives | +0.27% (z 3.0) | −0.46% (z −7.2) | −0.19% (z −2.1) |
| plays | −0.05% | −0.25% (z −8.1) | −0.30% (z −7.8) |
| sacks | +0.68% | −1.27% | −0.59% |
| giveaways | −0.13% | −1.21% | −1.34% (z −2.9) |
| points | −0.15% | −0.11% | −0.26% (z −1.5) |

C−A is positive because the **pools node** now looks wrong (section 6). G−C: the GLM shifts class mix by score and clock (more completions early, more incompletions late); seconds per snap rise +0.24 early and fall −0.34 in the late-half clock pools. Within a clock regime, per-class seconds barely move (|Δ| ≤ 0.01 s outside the late half). So fewer plays and drives are the GLM's score-aware mix meeting a regime-dependent clock. No metric moves more than 1.4%.

## 5. The score-and-clock gap in the simulation (VERIFIED shares; cause INFERRED)

Share of pass attempts, real 2023-25 vs the pools sim: early trailing by 8+ **16.1% vs 13.9%**; early within 8 **50.4% vs 52.8%**; late trailing 8+ 8.0% vs 7.5%. In the pools sim sacks do not vary with score (lead by 8+: 7.13%, real 6.07%, GLM 5.68%); the pool node was right in total and wrong by situation. INFERRED, untested: the sim's score margins are narrower than real (call or scoring dynamics upstream). A direct test is a margin-distribution comparison; not run.

## 6. Found: the pools node under-ends 4th-down drives (VERIFIED)

Real 2023-25 4th-down passes: records say the drive ends **65.5%** (2016-22: 65.4%); the pools node gives **46.3%** on those same states; the down-preserving sampler 63.0%. 4th-and-1 on the 3: pools node 0.571, correct ≈ 0.99. Cause: pools level 1 merges 3rd and 4th downs (36% of real 4th-down passes resolve there), so a failed 4th-down pass often continues the drive. Runs use the same hierarchy (not measured: UNKNOWN). The Python lab oracle has the same hierarchy, so conformance vectors agree with the bug.

## 7. Recommendation (mine, not yet done)

1. **GLM default:** explained, no bias; keep it off until the pools node's 4th-down fix lands so the baseline is fair, then switch the default in one commit. Not tuned to make the shift vanish.
2. **Fix the pools node:** apply the down-preserving hierarchy to every `rec` lookup (pass and run), in the Python oracle and TS together, regenerate the conformance vectors. Changes default sim output; I will report before and after.
3. **Check the margin distribution** (section 5) before the next call or score-state node.

## Limits
