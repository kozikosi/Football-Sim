## What it means (INFERRED)
1. **A repeatable per-offense and per-defense pass-class tilt exists in real data.** The offense effect is strong; the defense effect is real but weaker and noisier (split-half 0.3-0.4, year-to-year about 0.2). The two add: offense and defense slopes are each near 1 and statistically equal in 2023-2025.
2. **Slope about 1 is calibration, not discovery.** The gap is measured in the same units as the thing it predicts, so a well-shrunk gap should predict with slope near 1, and it does. So "lambda = 6.9" is the unit conversion `1 / Var_state(theta)` for a gap expressed as a mean tilt-score residual. It is NOT a free dial the Owner can tune to taste.
3. **The open problem is the mapping, not lambda.** The product has no per-team gap in r units. It has ratings. To use the tilt the game needs `passGap(offense, defense)` built from roster ratings (QB, receivers, protection vs pass rush, coverage). That mapping cannot be fit on history (no historical ratings in the repo); the only ratings file is Madden 27 (`data/nfl/madden27-ratings.csv`) with no games played on it. UNKNOWN: how much of the measured spread the ratings explain.
4. **Ex-ante vs in-season.** Leave-one-game-out uses the same season's other games, so this measures how much of a team-season's tilt is stable within the season. Persistence across seasons is lower (0.45 offense, 0.2 defense), which is the honest ceiling for a preseason-ratings gap.
