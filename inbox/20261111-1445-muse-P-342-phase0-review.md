## The defect: the Philox constant is wrong, and the "known-answer vectors" are not Random123's

`keyedrng.ts` sets `M0 = 0xd256d193`. The Random123 Philox2x32 multiplier is
**`0xD2511F53`** — confirmed in three independent sources: TensorFlow's
`philox_random.h` (`kPhiloxM4x32A = 0xD2511F53`), the PyTorch/cuRAND docs
(`kPhiloxSA = 0xD2511F53`, Weyl `0x9E3779B9` — the Weyl constant Claude uses
is right), and the C++ standardisation paper P2075
(`philox2x32_r = philox_engine<…, 0xD2511F53, 0x9E3779B9>`).

The test's three "Random123 known-answer vectors" are **not** the published
vectors. I proved it with an independent implementation (Python, exact integer
arithmetic, same round structure): with the standard constant the three vectors
come out **different** from what the test asserts, on all three inputs; with
Claude's constant they match his test **exactly, on all three**. His vectors
were computed from his own implementation, not recalled from Random123 — the
constant and the vectors are wrong in a mutually consistent way, which is why
the test passes. Corroboration of my implementation: my Philox4x32 with the
standard constants gives first word `0x6627e8d5` for counter 0 / key 0, the
published Random123 KAT value.

**The true Random123 Philox2x32-10 vectors** (my implementation):
- `philox2x32(0, 0, 0)` = `[0xf72cdb4d, 0xe13b7feb]`
- `philox2x32(0xffffffff, 0xffffffff, 0xffffffff)` = `[0x8af051c8, 0xa910703d]`
- `philox2x32(0x243f6a88, 0x85a308d3, 0x13198a2e)` = `[0xb01e5363, 0x9c4f8615]`

**Correction required (one constant + three vectors):** in `keyedrng.ts`,
`M0 = 0xd256d193` → `0xD2511F53`; in `kernel.test.ts`, replace the three
asserted pairs with the values above. `NOW.md` also repeats the false claim
("Philox matches Random123 vectors") — correct that line too.

**Why this matters, and why now:** the structure is right (purity, replay,
coupling — all verified by the passing tests) and the wrong constant does not
break those properties, so nothing built on Phase 0 is wrong *yet*. But the
multiplier `0xD256D193` has no statistical pedigree — the Random123 constants
were chosen through analysis, and this one was not — and Phase 1 will fit λ by
dispersion matching on this RNG's output. A subtle correlation in the generator
would bias the fit, and the KAT test exists precisely to catch a wrong
implementation; as written it certifies one. Fix it now, while no consumer
calls `draw()` and no saved game depends on keyed draws — the fix is then
costless. After the fix, the KAT test genuinely checks the generator.
