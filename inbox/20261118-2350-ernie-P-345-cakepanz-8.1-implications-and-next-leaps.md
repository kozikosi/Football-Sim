## 2. Idea A — the R-13 counterbalance is now *measurable*, not just small

The framework round (R-13, Block C / P-342) needs a sealed slice ported twice (Svelte → Vue) against the
unchanged TS core, with a number that decides. P-345 #11 argued the late-draft board is the right slice
because it is render-only (L4: VM sha identical across the flip) and small. 8.1 makes it stronger: the
slice now carries **nine laws (L1–L9) and four falsifiers (F13–F16)**, all written against `data-*` hooks
(P-345 #10), so they are **portable to the Vue arm unchanged**. The R-13 report should therefore be:

> "The Vue arm passes the same 47 shell laws + 9 late laws (L1–L9) and dies under the same F13–F16
> mutations, at the reported bundle bytes / boot-to-first-paint median of 5 / empty TS-core diff."

That is the counterbalance the Owner asked for: the framework choice stops being a taste call and becomes
"which arm passes the laws at the smaller bundle". The late-draft board is the **only** surface in the app
that already has a falsifier set; the full 60-row board has none. If the Owner wants the full board ported
instead, the late-draft numbers still transfer as the cheaper control — but the law-parity clause is the
part that makes the round decisive.

## 3. Idea B — a decision-cost law (upgrades P-345 #8; the law that would justify P-337 to the Owner)

8.1's new L7 tests that the detail is *present* one click away. It does **not** test that the simple form
is *easier* — which is the actual P-337 claim and the Owner's Q6 sentence ("not have to do complex math or
planning to make a decent roster"). Add **L-cost** (a new law, ~30 lines, real-UI):

- drive N picks (say 8) in each form from the same frozen store state;
- assert `clicks-to-pick(simple) ≤ clicks-to-pick(full)` (a click counter on the row), and
- assert `median wall-clock to confirm a pick (simple) ≤ (full)` (`performance.now()` around the confirm).

This turns "the board looks simpler" into "the board is measurably simpler" and hands the Owner a number to
judge the feature by. It is also the law that would have caught P-345 #2's two-click cost — which is why
idea C and this law belong in the same round.

## 4. Idea C — one-click pick in the simple form (P-345 #2, now unblocked)

The simple form still costs two clicks (`DRAFT` sets `picked`, a confirm appears, a second `DRAFT`). The
full form needs the confirm because the slot choice matters; the simple form does not (the slot is
`r.slotId`, the row says the position, and Undo is already loud in the footer). With F1/F2 fixed, make the
simple row's button draft immediately and let Undo be the safety net. This halves the late-draft
interaction cost and is the single largest UX win for the fewest lines — and it is what makes idea B's
`clicks-to-pick(simple) < clicks-to-pick(full)` assertion pass by construction.

## 5. Idea D — a colour gate (`gate-tokens.mjs`), so F3 cannot recur

F3 (the Block B literals) is a *process* failure, not a one-off: nothing stopped three files from
hard-coding the dark-mode golds. Add a sibling to `gate-numerals.mjs` that fails when a component's
`<style>` contains a hex literal equal to a token value (`#e8c87e`, `#e6c67d`, `#8a5f18`, `#9a6b1f`,
`#c39a4a`, `#ad7f2e`, `#c8a48f`-class) outside `tokens-*.css`. This generalises the numerals gate's
"no stale literal survives" philosophy to colour, and it is the guard that makes the Owner's team-takeover
ruling safe to ship: a component that cannot hard-code gold *cannot* accidentally sit out the takeover.
~20 lines, node builtins only, same exit discipline.

## 6. Idea E — a receipt-freshness convention (the L10 lesson, generalised)

The c8 numerals false-green (a receipt that said CLEAN while the gate failed at the correct pin) and the
L10 pointer (a receipt started but not finished) are the same class: **a receipt that does not name the
bytes it was run against can outlive its own tree**. Convention: every receipt header carries
`BYTES: <dist/single.html sha256>` and a `gate-receipts.mjs` checks that the sha in each `h*`/`k*`/`m*`
receipt equals the tree's current `dist/single.html` sha. Then "CLEAN ×2 both trees" can never be printed
over a tree whose bytes moved, and the F6 class (a gate green at the wrong pin) becomes impossible instead
of merely discouraged. This is the natural companion to the numerals gate and costs one more small script.

## 7. Idea F — the season hub simple mode (P-345 #9, still the Owner's call)

The Owner's sentence is about the draft, but the same arithmetic-avoidance argument covers the weekly hub:
one "recommended move" line ("start X over Y — 0.2 wins"), one "play next week" button, everything else one
click away. The simple-board work is the proof the pattern lands; the hub is where a user spends the other
17 weeks. This is a product call, not a layout one — flagged for the Owner, not proposed as a build.

## What I am NOT proposing

No engine change, no re-pin, no new flex price, no auto-draft (the Owner's Q6 asks for *simple* decisions,
not *no* decisions). Items B–E are all shell-layer and can land in one v8.2 with the F6/F3/F7 fixes from
the review; item A is a measurement protocol for R-13, not a build. Nothing here should be built before the
Owner rules on the two open contrast questions, because idea D and the F3 fix both touch the surfaces those
rulings will re-touch.
