## CURRENT RULES (2026 - aligned regular season and playoffs structure)

### Regular Season Overtime (2026)
From NFL rulebook/operations and current explanations:
- If tied at end of regulation, coin toss to determine possession (visitor calls). 3-minute intermission before OT.
- **10-minute period** (single period in regular season).
- **Both teams guaranteed a possession** unless the kicking team scores a safety on the receiving team's initial possession (game ends immediately).
- If first team scores a touchdown on opening drive: second team still gets possession. If second team scores touchdown and ties or takes lead (with 2-point conversion possible in some scenarios), game continues. If first team fails to score on opening drive or after both possessions score remains tied → **sudden death** (next score wins).
- Each team gets **2 timeouts**.
- **No coach's challenges** (replay official initiates reviews).
- **Point after try not attempted** if game ends on touchdown.
- If score still tied at end of 10-minute OT period → **game ends in a tie**.
- Source: NFL Operations Rulebook Rule 16, ESPN, Pro Football Network (2026)

### Playoff Overtime (2026)
- No ties possible; play continues until winner determined.
- Coin toss same as regular season.
- **Both teams get a possession** (guaranteed). If first team scores TD on opening drive, second team gets chance to respond.
- If tied after both possessions or second team's possession still in progress → play **another overtime period**.
- **15-minute periods**.
- **Two-minute intermission** between periods, no halftime after 2nd period.
- Each team gets **3 timeouts** per half.
- If tied after 2 OT periods → another coin toss, continue. Same timing rules as end of regulation apply.
- No coach's challenges (replay official reviews).
- Source: NFL Operations Rulebook, SB Nation, Pro Football Network (2026)

## FIRST-POSSESSION TOUCHDOWN CASE
**Regular season:** If receiving team scores TD on first possession, game does **not** end immediately (unlike old rules). Visiting/opposing team gets possession. If they score TD and tie or take lead via 2-point conversion in that scenario, play continues; if still tied after both possessions and no one won in sudden death, OT ends and game is a tie (if time expires). If second team fails to score and first team doesn't score again in sudden death, result depends - but both had possession. More precisely: "Both teams must possess, or have the opportunity to possess, the ball..." so first-possession TD means game continues to second possession; sudden death applies only after both have had possession and it's still tied.
**Playoffs:** Same - first-possession TD does **not** end game; second team gets possession. Game continues until winner determined.

## FIELD GOALS
**Regular season:** If first team kicks FG on opening drive, second team gets possession. If second team scores TD → they win (game ends). If second team kicks FG → tied, then sudden death next score wins. If second team fails to score and time expires tied → tie.
**Playoffs:** If first team FG, second team gets possession; if second team scores TD → wins, if FG → tied and game continues to sudden death/next OT periods as needed.

## SAFETIES
**Regular season:** If kicking team scores a safety on the receiving team's initial possession, game ends immediately (kicking team wins). Otherwise safeties can occur during play.
**Playoffs:** Similar - safety can end game in appropriate circumstances.

## TIES
**Regular season:** Possible if tied at end of 10-minute OT period.
**Playoffs:** No ties - play continues to additional OT periods until winner determined.
