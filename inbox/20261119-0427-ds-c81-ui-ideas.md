IDEA filing per P-345's shape: what to build, why it matters, where it lives, what it costs, what law proves
it. Nothing here is an assigned task; the Owner and the Architect decide.

**The one-line thesis.** The shell has already won the hard argument — it is one 515 KB HTML file, offline,
deterministic, with a pure board VM — and it is now spending that advantage on *modes* (full/simple, 15 nav
tabs) instead of on the *two things a draft is*: a decision you make 32 times and a team you watch take shape.
The next round of value is not another view; it is making the one decision in front of the user feel inevitable,
and making the team's shape visible while it happens.

## A · The decision (the board)

### A1. The Pick Card — a depth-0 view for phones and the late draft (best single idea)
**What.** One player, full width: name, pos, NFL team, OVR, the one number that matters for this sort, gone%,
*fills* in words ("fills QB — your 3rd need"), and one primary button ("Draft Burrow at QB"). "Next 5" below as
compact rows; a swipe/arrow moves the card. It is the same `boardVM.rows`, slice `[0]` and `[1..5]`.
**Why.** At 390 px a full row is 100+ px tall and names wrap mid-word ("Caleb Willia / ms"); the simple row is
still a list of 60 rows at roughly 45 px each — a wall either way. Late in a 32-round draft the user is not shopping, they are
confirming: the decision is "who", not "the list". The card also makes the flip physical: full board → card at
pick 10, one control back to the list.
**Where.** New `PickCard.svelte`; `DraftBoard` renders it when a `view` prop says `card` (presentation only —
the L4 sha law must keep the VM identical). `App.svelte` owns the view choice.
**Cost.** Medium (one component + CSS + the view switch). No engine contact.
**Law.** Card renders `rows[0]`'s fields verbatim; a pick from the card lands and autosaves; 390 px no scroll;
the VM sha is identical in card and list at the same state (extend L4).

### A2. Alarm-only gone% — a percentage only when it can change the pick
**What.** Rename the column in the mind of the user: it answers "will he last to my next pick?". Show the number
only when it is informative (say ≥ 70%), as a small chip ("87% gone"); below that a quiet dash. Keep the exact
value in the row detail and on hover for the curious.
**Why.** The board currently prints 99% on Burrow and 0% on a run of players; a repeated 0% column teaches the
eye to skip the column entirely, which is where the shell's most distinctive information lives. A signal that is
always on is never read.
**Where.** `BoardRow.svelte` (`r.gone` string is already formatted); the VM keeps the full value.
**Cost.** Trivial (formatting + CSS).
**Law.** Zero-risk rows render the dash; ≥70% rows render the chip with the VM's own number; the detail always
shows the number and the method.

### A3. Label the numbers — a header row and the missing WINS in the detail
**What.** A sticky header over the full board: `OVR · WINS · PRIO · GONE%` with the same captions the simple row
carries, and add WINS to the simple row's detail line (it is the only one of the three numbers the detail omits).
**Why.** Three unlabelled right-aligned numbers is the single biggest scanability gap I saw in the shell. The
caption work in 8.1 was done *only* in simple mode; the full board is where numbers get compared.
**Where.** `BoardHeader.svelte` / `DraftBoard.svelte`; `BoardRow.svelte`'s `cp-fitlbl`.
**Cost.** Trivial.
**Law.** The header's labels match SortControls; the simple detail carries all three numbers.

### A4. Keyboard-first drafting
**What.** `j/k` (or ↑/↓) moves a focused row, `d` arms the pick, `Enter` confirms, `Esc` cancels, `/` focuses the
needs filter, `f` toggles FULL BOARD. A focused-row outline and a small "keys" hint on first visit.
**Why.** 32 rounds × 2–3 clicks is a keyboard job on a desktop; every competitive draft tool is keyboard-driven.
The rows are already `<button>`s, so the focus model exists — this is mostly a keydown handler and a focus ring.
**Where.** `DraftBoard.svelte` (`onkeydown` at the section, `tabindex`), `App.svelte` for the view toggle.
**Cost.** Small-medium.
**Law.** `j j d Enter` makes a pick; the confirm is never skipped; the focus order matches the visual order.

### A5. The flip as a fold, not a swap
**What.** Animate the 10th-pick transition: the three numbers fold into the one, the advice panel collapses to
the shape strip (B1), over ~300 ms; suppressed by `prefers-reduced-motion` (the shell already reads it).
**Why.** The current flip changes the anatomy of every row with one line of prose. A transformation the user
sees teaches where the numbers went; a silent swap reads as a bug ("my board broke").
**Where.** CSS transitions on the row grid + a `data-flipping` attribute for one frame; no new state.
**Cost.** Small.
**Law.** Under reduced motion the flip is instant; the end states are exactly L1's; no layout shift at 390 px.

### A6. One name on the screen
**What.** Header reads "76 Pancakes"; "67 Cakepanz · the shell" moves to the dev overlay (`?dev=1`) only.
**Why.** Two names for one product on the first screen is the cheapest trust leak in the build. AGENTS.md §2 is
explicit that the product is 76 Pancakes and Cakepanz is the track.
**Where.** `App.svelte` header.
**Cost.** Trivial.
**Law.** The name is absent outside dev; present in dev.

## B · The team (the shape you are building)

### B1. A "your draft" strip — the shape stays visible when the advice panel goes
**What.** One horizontal strip under the board in simple mode: `QB ✓ · RB ✓ · WR ✓ · TE — · OT ✓ …` (10–12
slot chips, filled vs open, tap → roster). It replaces the vanished `LineupAdvice` panel with the *information*
(which holes remain) and none of the math.
**Why.** P-337's whole point is "simple decisions in the second half", and the current simple mode deletes the
only view of the team's shape at exactly the moment the user needs it ("do I have a TE yet?"). The strip is
smaller than the advice panel and answers the only question the late rounds really ask.
**Where.** New `ShapeStrip.svelte` reading `boardVM.header`/roster; no engine change.
**Cost.** Small.
**Law.** After N picks the strip's filled count equals the user's picks by slot; a tap opens the roster; 390 px
no scroll; absent in full mode (it is the advice panel's replacement there).

### B2. The Complete screen is a card you can send
**What.** The draft-complete screen renders the shareable card (`shareable.ts` already exists and the battery
covers it) with two buttons: "Copy image" and "Download .html card" (self-contained, no CDN — the N14 law).
**Why.** A finished 32-round draft is the most shareable artefact this product produces, and it is currently a
table with a stamp. The offline single-file constraint makes the *file* the natural share unit.
**Where.** `Complete.svelte` (or wherever the record renders) + the existing `shareable` port.
**Cost.** Small.
**Law.** The downloaded file opens offline and shows the same record; no network request on render (N14).

### B3. Saves as files — the single HTML becomes a console, not a page
**What.** Export the autosave to a `.json` file; drop a save onto the page to open it (`dragover` + `FileReader` →
the same `restore()` path); a "take this draft to another machine" pair of buttons.
**Why.** The envelope is already portable and versioned; the product's differentiator is "one file, no account".
Save files are the natural continuation of that promise, and they make the Draft Board demo-able in a room with
no network.
**Where.** `savesApp.ts`/`hydrateApp.ts` (serialize/restore already exist) + a dropzone in `Saves.svelte`.
**Cost.** Small (plumbing); the corrupt-never-repair rule already covers bad files.
**Law.** Export → clear → import restores the same record sha; a corrupt file is refused with the engine's own
message (never repaired).

## C · The draft as a thing that can be linked, replayed, and measured

### C1. Deep links that reproduce a pick ("you're on the clock at 71")
**What.** Put the seed and the pick number in the hash: `#/board?seed=4821&pick=71`; opening it boots to that
exact state (the CPU is deterministic). A "copy this pick" button beside the header. Today `startDraft` uses
`Math.random()` for the seed, so nothing is reproducible.
**Why.** "Look at this board — who would you take?" is the most natural viral unit the game has, and the
single-file build keeps the link working offline. It also makes bug reports one click ("here is my board") for
the Architect.
**Where.** `App.svelte` seed (read from hash, else random), `router.ts` (carry params), the engine's store
(already deterministic given seed + picks).
**Cost.** Small-medium; the only real cost is deciding that a seed is part of the URL contract.
**Law.** The same URL twice produces the same board VM sha; a bad/absent seed falls back to random; the hash
door still converges on Complete (L8's leg).

### C2. Golden board shas — "no engine number moved" across rounds, not just within a session
**What.** A small committed fixture: `(seed, userTeam, pickIndex) → boardVM sha`, asserted in the node battery.
L4 proves presentation-only *within* a session; the fixture proves it *across* rounds and catches an engine
drift that lands between two deliveries.
**Why.** Every cakepanz round rewrites the board's presentation; without a golden reference, the only guard is a
re-run of the engine suite, which tests the engine, not the shell's consumption of it.
**Where.** `batteries/fixtures/board-golden.json` + one test.
**Cost.** Trivial.
**Law.** The fixture matches on the pinned engine; a deliberate engine change updates the fixture in the same
commit and the diff is reviewable.

### C3. A persistence state machine (the D1 lesson, generalised)
**What.** One `persist.ts`: `saveDraft()`, `saveWeek()`, `clearWeek(reason: 'new-draft' | 'slot-load')`, and the
rule that *only an explicit new draft or a slot load may clear a save*. `App.svelte` calls it; the boot path can
never delete the thing it is about to resume.
**Why.** D1 exists because "who may clear the week" is implicit in call order. The shell now has four writers
(draft autosave, week autosave, slots, boot) and three clearers; the fix is small and the next bug of this family
is already visible ("loadDraft from the Saves panel clears the week even when the week belongs to the same
lineage").
**Where.** `src/lib/port/persist.ts` (new), `App.svelte` call sites.
**Cost.** Small; one behaviour decision (slot load clears, boot keeps — my proven `keepWeek` is the same idea).
**Law.** After every boot the week save still exists (S29b); after a new draft it does not; after a slot load of a
different lineage it does not.

## D · What I would not do

- **Do not add a fourth view.** Two boards + a card is the ceiling; every extra toggle multiplies the state the
  laws must cover — the 8.1 round shipped one toggle and grew two defects.
- **Do not hide gone% entirely.** It is the shell's most distinctive number (the engine computes it; no other
  draft tool does). Make it a signal, not a column of noise.
- **Do not use long-press for the method.** Invisible gestures are not discoverable; the method belongs in the
  detail as a sentence (D2's fix).
- **Do not make the Owner's preset a modal.** It is one click today; keep it one click.

## E · Implications for the Architect's engine / the cutover

1. **The shell is proving the engine's ergonomics, not just its numbers.** Two shell defects in 8.1 come from
   *lifecycle* (what survives a reload), not from the board; the engine's `serialize/restore` contract is already
   the right shape, so the shell needs a matching lifecycle contract (C3) before cutover.
2. **Determinism is now a product feature, not just a test property.** C1 needs the seed to be a user-visible
   value; the same change makes the Architect's seeded before/after discipline (AGENTS.md §3) easier: a bug
   report can carry the URL instead of a prose description.
3. **The two-tree harness (P-291) should run the same browser laws.** If Colors and Cakepanz are compared with
   the same shell laws (47 + 9), cutover evidence becomes mechanical; today the browser laws live only in
   Cakepanz's tree.
4. **The golden fixture (C2) is the cheap bridge** between the Architect's engine pin and Z.ai's presentation
   track: it catches "the shell reads an engine number that moved" without either tree owning the other.
