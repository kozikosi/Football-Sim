## Q1 — Derivation formula

Proposed: **penalty(src, dst) = round_to_tier( A(src, dst_role) × L(dst_slot) )**, tiers {0,2,6,12}, where

- A(src, dst_role) = athletic-distance base, already owned by the D-picks (0/6/12 tiers: 0 same family, 6 adjacent, 12 cross-family). This is the existing ability picture the Owner approved; the formula does not re-derive it.
- L(dst_slot) = destination leverage, derived from the destination slot's weight and role family:
  - L = 1.0 for coverage destinations (CB/FS/SS/NICKEL/DIME and any slot with weight ≥ 100 that is a defensive-back family member) — athletic ability is the binding constraint; misfits here are real.
  - L = 0.5 for low-weight utility destinations (weight ≤ 50 that are NOT coverage: TE3, WR4, FB, K, P) — a misfit into a 50-weight utility spot costs little to team value, so the penalty is halved one tier.
- DIME is the one explicit coverage override at weight 50: L(DIME)=1.0. Justification: DIME is a sixth DB, not a utility slot; a TE/ILB/OLB in DIME is a coverage mismatch, not a low-leverage fill. This is why TE>DIME, ILB>DIME, OLB>DIME stay at −12 under the formula (A=12 × 1.0 = 12).

Key pairs produced:
- TE>DIME / ILB>DIME / OLB>DIME: 12 × 1.0 = 12 (unchanged — matches Owner's ruling).
- FB>TE (into TE3, weight 50, utility): 6 × 0.5 = 3 → rounds down to 2 (matches the approved 6→2).
- FB>TE (into TE1/TE2, weight ≥150): 6 × 1.0 = 6 (unchanged — high-weight destination, full penalty).
- TE>DIME stays 12; TE>FS stays 12 (FS weight 300, coverage family, L=1.0).
- WR>DIME (weight 50, coverage): 6 × 1.0 = 6 (unchanged under this formula; if the Owner wants WR>DIME reduced, classify WR-destinations as utility — a one-line rule change).

Better derivation if the Owner prefers weight-only (no family table): L(w) = min(1, w/100) with a hard floor L(DIME)=1.0. This removes the family lookup entirely and makes the table a pure function of (A, w). The family version above is more defensible (DIME really is coverage); the weight-only version is more portable. I prefer the family version but flag the trade-off.

Falsifier: if the formula produces a penalty that disagrees with the Owner's approved table on more than 3 of the 99 rules, the derivation is wrong, not the table.

## Q2 — Optimizer math verification

Verified by reading colors/src/engine/lineup.ts + src/core/hungarian.ts (commit 6dcf13b). The solver:

1. Builds a square (n+s)×(n+s) cost matrix: n player rows + s empty-spot filler rows; s spot columns + n bench columns.
2. Each player×spot cell = -(PLAYS + unit×(ovr − penalty)) + emergency, or ILLEGAL (1e12) if the flex is illegal. Filler×spot = -unit×EMPTY_OVR. Bench columns cost 0.
3. Runs the Hungarian algorithm (potentials method, O(n³)), which computes the exact minimum-cost perfect matching on a square matrix — i.e. the true optimum, not a heuristic. Deterministic (ties by index order).
4. The PLAYS (1e7) / EMERGENCY (1e5) / ILLEGAL (1e12) constants enforce lexicographic priorities (QB never unlisted, everyone plays, fewest emergencies) before value, because each dominates everything below it in magnitude.

Ways it could be wrong:
- (a) Priority-scale overlap: a spot value (≤1000) × OVR (≤100) × PLAYS (1e7) = up to ~1e12, which sits at ILLEGAL's magnitude (1e12). A very high-value, high-OVR, non-emergency fill could in principle collide with the illegal sentinel. In practice the max product is ~1e10, two orders below 1e12, so no collision today — but the margin is thin and a future weight or OVR change could break it. Recommend raising ILLEGAL to 1e15 or using a separate feasibility flag.
- (b) The matrix assumes each player fills at most one spot and each spot at most one player (Hungarian guarantees this). Correct for a lineup.
- (c) Empty spots are filled with a 70-OVR replacement; if EMPTY_OVR is wrong the optimum shifts. It is a constant, not measured.
- (d) The base is fixed by the caller; the solver does not compare 4-3 vs 3-4 totals (by design, Owner 2 Nov). If that ruling ever changes, lineupFor must be called twice and compared — the code does not do it.
- (e) Numerical: sums of ~1e10 in double precision are exact to ~1e-6, fine; no precision issue at this scale.

Conclusion: the optimizer is exact (Hungarian) and the math is sound; the only real risk is the ILLEGAL sentinel's thin margin (a).
