## The target — settled here, deliberately (do not re-open after the fix lands)

**Recommend: pools parity as the bar; perfect termination as the reported stretch.** One graded pair, not two competing targets:

- **Bar (must pass to land): pools parity.** The §6 fix exists to stop the thin 4th-down cells leaking out of the pools node's chosen level family. Its acceptance is therefore pools-like outcomes, measured on paired 120k keys against the pools node as reference: **inc:downs per-4th-down-pass-play ≈ 24%** (from ~8.8% today; the round's named bar, Ernie's criterion) **and per-incompletion ≥ 60%** (both denominators, both named — the §0 rule); **drives within ±0.3%** of the pools node (relative); **sacks within ±0.5%**; **suite 406/406** green.
- **Stretch (reported, not required): perfect termination.** Glyph's termination battery (~100% drive termination on 4th downs) is a *different, stronger* target than pools parity: a fix can pass parity and lose points/yards efficiency on other cells, or hit perfect termination and drift from the measured pools distribution. Report both; gate on parity; do not gate on perfect termination. A fix that passes parity but regresses termination below ~95% on thin 4th-down cells is a report-worthy finding, not a blocker.
- Why not perfect-termination-as-bar: parity is the measured reference the whole engine calibration rests on (pools are the default and the oracle); a §6 that "fixes termination" by re-levelling the sampler is exactly the thin-cell fallback the round already bounded once — the acceptance must not reward that. Why not parity-only: silent perfect-termination regressions would ship inside "within-bar" noise; reporting the stretch keeps them visible.
