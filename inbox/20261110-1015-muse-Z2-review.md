## 3. The C>OT exit-premium reading — the most important new idea in the package

Z.ai's §2 is the strongest conceptual work in the delivery, and it changes how the C>OT residual should be understood:
- **The ISOP test is a genuine negative result.** Z.ai built the one-on-one duty seriously (it fixes C>OT, C>OG, TE>OT, self-doable OK) and it still fails — because the same ISOP deficit that makes C>OT cost 12 also makes DT>OT, EDGE>OT, DT>OG, EDGE>OG, TE>OG cost 12, and the Owner prices every DL→OL entry at 6. A duty that fixes the target row and breaks five others is not the mechanism.
- **The reason it had to fail: C>OT is an EXIT premium, not a job demand.** Compare two rows into the same destination job: DT>OT (a 5-PBLK body, worse prepared) costs 6; C>OT (a 9-PBLK body, better prepared) costs 12. If C>OT priced the OT job's demands, the worse body would cost more. It does not. **The 12 is about what you lose at center** — the SNP 10 hub, the line calls, the protection slides everyone else lines up by. A deficit-vs-destination formula is structurally blind to exit premiums, which is exactly why ZAI-D1 has no fiat rows.
- **Z.ai's proposal: adopt V4 as the working derivation and accept C>OT as the one named, principled off-by-one.** Reasons: (1) the Owner's own body score (6–7) is more credible than the 4 that would force the fit — deflating the center to 4 would bend the ability model to catch one row; (2) the residual is *explained*, not just tolerated. "A model with 0 fiat rows that names its one honest miss is worth more than a 99/99 with a hidden patch." MUSE concurs.
- **The exit-premium surface (tested, Owner's call):** one authored scalar per role, `EXIT[role]`, added to the raw on all exits from that role (FB/specialist exits exempt — preserves C>FB = 0 and the floor's FB exemption symmetry). `EXIT[C] = 3.0` on top of V4: **99/99, tier-error 0** — C>OT 9.68 → 12 ✓, C>OG 8.13 → 6 ✓, C>DT 12.12 → 12 ✓, C>FB 0 ✓. But it is a formula extension, so it is the Owner's call: **ship 98/99 now; the surface is one line of code if he says the word.** This is now an open Owner decision (§2.3).

## 5. The five innovations (§4) — what to carry forward

- **§4.1 Derive the duty list itself (NEED-support procedure):** author the jobs first, then let the duties be the support of the NEED matrix — any demand-axis with variance across jobs is a duty; any axis where every job demands the same level prices nothing and is dropped; any axis only one job loads is a scoped duty. Testable claim: re-running this on football recovers a matrix within one tier of ZAI-D1's 98/99. **This is the most actionable innovation for Claude's merged design — it is a procedure for authoring his duty table once, rather than authoring duties by hand.**
- **§4.2 Six primitive families (CREATE, FINISH, PROTECT, CONNECT, RECOVER, PROJECT):** a shared duty vocabulary for onboarding new sports. Testable claim: basketball needed zero new primitives.
- **§4.3 The hub-exit premium is the multisport bridge:** a hub role is the role whose exit re-organizes the unit (football C, basketball PG). Prediction: if basketball ever gets a live table, the PG is where the Owner's hand shows the same pattern. If that prediction lands, the exit-premium surface earns its place in the formula; if not, it was football-specific and the off-by-one stands. Either way the formula tells us something falsifiable.
- **§4.4 Where the formula bends in fluid sports (named honestly):** lineup geometry (five-man units), foul exclusion (the sixth foul, unpriced), two-way jobs (every basketball job loads both ends), position continua (jobs are fixed demand profiles even when bodies are fluid). Named, not solved — the right scope for a design sketch.
- **§4.5 No basketball duty with no football equivalent:** the honest answer is no — HREP is the EREP analogue, FT is the SNP analogue, OFFB is RTE off the ball, SHOT3 is DEEP with a floor. What basketball has that football lacks is structure (foul exclusion, lineup geometry), not a duty. "The duty vocabulary is sport-portable; the sport-ness lives in the floors, the job authoring and the calibration set."
