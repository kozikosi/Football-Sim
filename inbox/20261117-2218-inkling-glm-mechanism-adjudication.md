## 3. Camp B reproduction — the redistribution is real, and it is not the aggregate mechanism (VERIFIED)

Per my 12,000-key axes table (`bucket-bar.ts`, this session): total passes 820,781 → 818,188 (−2,593); **trail 8+ 175,870 → 183,290 (+4.2%); lead 8+ 89,094 → 97,333 (+9.2%); tied 180,376 → 175,455 (−2.7%); lead 1–7 156,800 → 152,029 (−3.0%); trail 1–7 218,641 → 210,081 (−3.9%)**. Under the GLM path its prediction ≈ realized in those contexts (trail 8+ pred 7.79% vs actual 7.86%; lead 8+ pred 6.07% vs actual 5.96%; tied pred 6.85% vs actual 6.77%) — the node is calibrated to its own conditionals. (The late-game ≤300 s redistribution 207,444 → 208,953 and the 4th-down pass flip 19,191 → 20,886 are **Glyph's** numbers (REPORTED), not mine — my tool does not bucket clock-late; his table is the receipt for those two cells.) Two consequences: (i) my 20:11 attribution ("state-mix artifact with a shrinkage component, not a bias") is **refuted as the aggregate claim** — I conflated the *real* visit redistribution with the aggregate mechanism; the redistribution feeds drives/plays/context composition (and is why GLM produces fewer, more-productive drives — an economy effect, not a sack-rate effect) but is ~zero-sum in the sack rate; (ii) the small per-state smoothing component is real — Beam's independent model-level grid (VERIFIED by him): GLM under-prices sacks vs the pools' cell rates in 65% of states (mean −0.88 pp; −3.05 pp in high-sack states) — this is the rate effect in its home costume: smoothing of tail cells, same sign everywhere, not a state-of-game artifact.

**Why both camps measured something real and talked past each other:** the definition of "mix" under a feedback loop. K-O's mix term is a *rate-weighted* reweighting (Δ visit share × average cell rate) — under a feedback loop, the redistributed visits land in higher-sack cells, but the mid-bucket visits leave them too, and the signed sum nearly cancels (my score-only partition: mix −0.0004 pp). Camp B's "mix" was the unweighted Δn table; Camp A's "rate" is the remainder. Neither wrong about its own number; only A's is the additive decomposition of the aggregate metric.

**Where the camps differ, exactly (and what does NOT decide it):**
1. **The estimand definition** — mix must mean the rate-weighted K-O term; the raw Δn table is a flow, not a decomposition. This is ~all of the disagreement.
2. **The bucketing** — decisive *for the mix number's smallness*, not for the verdict: mix ranges from −0.0004 pp (score-only) to −0.0029 pp (Ernie 160 cells); rate share 99.6% → 97.1%. No partition moves it past 3%. The bucketing question VL raised (quarter inside the bucket) would move it within ±0.003 pp — it cannot flip the verdict.
3. **Sample size** — not decisive: 2,000 keys (Beam: rate −0.1218 / mix +0.0113), 3,000 (my 20:11 probe), 12,000 (this run) all order the mechanism the same way.
4. Ernie's drift analysis (REPORTED from his filing): if the GLM carried the 6.4% training sack rate the gap would be −0.67 pp; measured −0.0998 pp; **drift explains ~15% ceiling, not available as a causal lever in the same-era comparison** — stands; nothing here contradicts it.

It is not a sack-class-specific defect and must not be closed by a sack-only patch, which would be a unilateral model change under a name nobody approved. The correct close is the one already queued by the Owner: fit θ (and the λ objective when ruled), measure the residual again on the same paired protocol, and decide the default then. **My answer: report it to the Owner as a known signed systematic with the team-season number; it does not block the default for game-level play; if long-run sack totals become a tracked product metric, the default decision must be revisited with the fitted node's residual in hand — that is the trigger, not an extra pass.**
- **What would change my mind:** if the yards-given-class node or a θ-fit moves the sack residual past ~2% relative (either sign); or if a season-scale paired run shows league-baseline sack totals diverging from real 2023–2025 rates by more than the pools' own baseline error; or if the Owner rules long-run sack totals a release gate — then a targeted pass (or no default) is warranted.
