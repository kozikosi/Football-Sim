## 2. New check — C2's real-data D4 diagnostic is mislabelled

Review 1 and 2 both verified C2's JSON against the script; neither asked what the field means. `c2_lab_and_validation.py:196` defines

`dispersion_matching_overstatement_ratio = sqrt(total_var / within_var)`

where `within_var` is the variance **within drive-count buckets**. That is the share of points variance *not* explained by drive count — a drive-count R² diagnostic (1.0024 means drive count explains 0.5% of variance). It is not the D4 bias: D4 is the overstatement from attributing the **residual** variance to ratings, so the relevant denominator is the between-team component, not the within-drive-count one.

Recomputed from MUSE's P-344 file (`team_game_summary_2016-2025.csv`, 5,522 team-games, 320 team-seasons):

| estimator | formula | overstatement of λ |
|---|---|---|
| team-season dispersion matching | `sqrt(Var(team means) / (Var(team means) − mean within-game var / n̄))` | **×1.184 (+18.4%)** |
| game-level dispersion matching | `sqrt(total var / true between var)` | **×2.82 (+182%)** |
| C2's delivered field | `sqrt(total / within-by-drives)` | ×1.0024 (**not D4**) |

Per season the team-season figure is 1.160 (2016), 1.208 (2020), 1.199 (2025); true between-team variance is 11.1–13.1 points², within-game variance 75.7–87.9, n̄ ≈ 17. The direction confirms D4; the magnitude the design should fear is **game-level**, not season-level, and the delivered 1.0024 is not comparable to either.

**This also yields a cleaner estimator than the one proposed in P-347 (filed separately as an innovation note):** a one-way variance-components (ANOVA) estimator, `λ̂² = (Var(team means) − mean within-game var / n̄) / Var(g)`, is the same moment regression in disguise, needs no simulator, and is *exactly* unbiased for balanced panels. It is the analytic form of "simulated dispersion matching", with the model's own noise subtracted instead of simulated. The only extra input over the design's wording is games played per team (already in the P-344 file).
