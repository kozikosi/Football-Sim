## 1. NFL Playoff Structure (2020–present, 14-team format)

- **Total teams:** 14 (7 per conference: AFC, NFC)
- **Division winners:** 4 per conference → seeds 1–4 (by record, not automatic top 4; division winners always seeded ahead of wild cards even with worse record)
- **Wild card teams:** 3 per conference → seeds 5–7 (best records among non-division winners)
- **First-round byes:** Seeds 1–2 only (2 byes per conference, not 4)
- **Wild card round:** Seeds 3–7 enter
- **Reseeding:** Not used; bracket is fixed after seeding
- **Home-field:** Higher seed hosts (Super Bowl neutral site)

## 2. Official NFL Tiebreaker Order (from NFL Rulebook / NFL.com)

### Within Division (2 clubs)
1. Head-to-head (best won-lost-tied % in games between the clubs)
2. Best won-lost-tied % in games played within the division
3. Best won-lost-tied % in common games
4. Best won-lost-tied % in games played within the conference
5. Strength of victory in all games
6. Strength of schedule in all games
7. Best combined ranking among conference teams in points scored and points allowed in all games
8. Best combined ranking among all teams in points scored and points allowed in all games
9. Best net points in common games
10. Best net points in all games
11. Best net touchdowns in all games
12. Coin toss

### Within Division (3+ clubs)
*If two clubs remain tied after one-or-more clubs are eliminated during any step, tiebreaker restarts at Step 1 of two-club format. If three clubs remain tied after a fourth club is eliminated during any step, tiebreaker restarts at Step 1 of three-club format.*
1. Head-to-head (best won-lost-tied % in games among the clubs)
2. Best won-lost-tied % in games played within the division
3. Best won-lost-tied % in common games
4. Best won-lost-tied % in games played within the conference
5. Strength of victory in all games
6. Strength of schedule in all games
7. Best combined ranking among conference teams in points scored and points allowed in all games
8. Best combined ranking among all teams in points scored and points allowed in all games
9. Best net points in common games
10. Best net points in all games
11. Best net touchdowns in all games
12. Coin toss

### For Wild Card (different divisions)
**Two clubs:**
1. Head-to-head, if applicable
2. Best won-lost-tied % in games played within the conference
3. Best won-lost-tied % in common games, minimum of four
4. Strength of victory in all games
5. Strength of schedule in all games
6. Best combined ranking among conference teams in points scored and points allowed in all games
7. Best combined ranking among all teams in points scored and points allowed in all games
8. Best net points in conference games
9. Best net points in all games
10. Best net touchdowns in all games
11. Coin toss

**Three+ clubs:**
1. Apply division tiebreaker to eliminate all but the highest ranked club in each division prior to proceeding to step 2. The original seeding within a division upon application of the division tiebreaker remains the same for all subsequent applications of the procedure that are necessary to identify the two Wild-Card participants.
2. Head-to-head sweep. (Applicable only if one club has defeated each of the others or if one club has lost to each of the others.)
3. Best won-lost-tied % in games played within the conference
4. Best won-lost-tied % in common games, minimum of four
5. Strength of victory in all games
6. Strength of schedule in all games
7. Best combined ranking among conference teams in points scored and points allowed in all games
8. Best combined ranking among all teams in points scored and points allowed in all games
9. Best net points in conference games
10. Best net points in all games
11. Best net touchdowns in all games
12. Coin toss

*When the first Wild Card team has been identified, the procedure is repeated to name the second and third Wild Card.*

### Other Tie-Breaking Procedures
- Only one club advances to the playoffs in any tie-breaking step. Remaining tied clubs revert to the first step of the applicable division or Wild Card tie-breakers.
- In comparing records against common opponents among tied teams, the best won-lost-tied percentage is the deciding factor (teams may have played an unequal number of games).
- To determine home-field priority among division winners, apply Wild Card tiebreakers.
- To determine home-field priority for Wild Card qualifiers, apply division tiebreakers (if teams are from the same division) or Wild Card tiebreakers (if teams are from different divisions).
- To determine the best combined ranking among conference teams in points scored and points allowed: add a team's position in the two categories; the lowest score wins. If two teams are tied for a position, both teams are awarded the ranking as if they held it solely.

## 3. 17-Game Schedule Rules (2021–present)

Each team plays 17 games:
- **6 division games** (3 home, 3 away — "intraconference / interdivision" games)
- **4 conference games** vs same division of opposite conference (e.g., AFC North vs NFC North, NFC South vs AFC South — 1 each from the paired division's 4 teams)
- **4 non-conference games** (2 home, 2 away — rotating)
- **3 inter-conference division games** (1 home, 2 away or 2 home, 1 away — the "9th road game")
- **Total:** 17 games (not all teams play same number of home games — varies 8–9)

## 5. Key Observations for Engine Integration

1. **Division winner ≠ best record:** Multiple cases where a division winner had a worse record than a wild card team from another division (e.g., 2020 NFC East WAS 7-9 won division over TB 11-5 and LAR 10-6 wild cards)
2. **Three-way division ties occur:** 2022 AFC North had CIN/BAL/PIT all at 3-3 division record; 2022 NFC South had TB/CAR/NO/ATL all at 7-10 or 8-9
3. **Common games matter:** The tiebreaker requires minimum 4 common games for wild card comparisons
4. **Strength of Victory/Schedule:** Requires full opponent record tracking across all 17 games
5. **Conference ranking (points scored/allowed):** Requires league-wide ranking computation
