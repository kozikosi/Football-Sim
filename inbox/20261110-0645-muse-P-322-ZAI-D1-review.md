## Verdict: improved

Z.ai designed his own derivation (ZAI-D1, "the load-bearing deficit") instead of re-implementing Inkling's, per the Owner's directive. It is a genuine independent design — a different shape (max-gap + discounted sum over 17 scoped duties, NEED/HAVE split, no D term, no overrides, no U-deltas, 0 fiat rows) — and it fits the live 99-rule table at **95 exact / 3 off-by-one / 1 hard / tier-error 5**. It does not beat MUSE's R4 (99/99) or E5 (97/99) on raw fit, but it is the most principled derivation on the table: the only one with zero fiat rows, and the only one that prices DL5 by duty rather than by neighborhood. It also closes a loop the council had open (he ran Claude's `flexderive.mjs` himself and reproduced Inkling's 88/11/0 and 93/6/0) and contributes two findings that stand on their own (the quasi-metric audit and the DL5 NEED profile).

## Two findings that stand on their own

1. **The live table is a directed quasi-metric, and its 13 triangle violations are exactly the Owner's hand-set premium rows.** TE>RB, TE>OT, TE>FS, TE>DIME, C>OT, EDGE>SS, EDGE>RB, ILB>NICKEL, ILB>DIME, ILB>FS, OLB>DIME, OLB>FS, FS>OLB — each priced above what the rest of the table implies. This gives the council a free audit tool: any future hand-set price that breaks the triangle is a deliberate premium, and any that doesn't is derivable from the table it sits in. This finding does not depend on ZAI-D1 winning — it is useful regardless.
2. **The DL5 NEED profile.** Under the Owner's blitzer reframing, DL5 is priced by duty, not by neighborhood: EDGE 0, DT 2, OLB/ILB 2, OT/OG 6, SS/TE 12. The current live rule (cheaper of role→DT and role→EDGE) prices DL5 by neighborhood; Z.ai's prices come out the same or one tier different everywhere the neighborhood logic was already right, but the mechanism generalizes — a future DL6 or SAM-only package slot is one NEED dict, not a new neighbor rule. Note: these DL5 prices are Z.ai's model outputs, not Owner-ratified prices. The Owner should rule on IDEA-3 (NEED-first slot authoring) before DL5 is priced this way.
