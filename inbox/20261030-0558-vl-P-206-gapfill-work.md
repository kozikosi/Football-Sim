FIELD-GOAL RULE TEXT IN OVERTIME — VERIFIED:

Regular season (2026, both-possess rule, 10-minute period, can tie):
- Source: 2025 NFL Playing Rules Proposal No. 3 (media.nfl.com) + ESPN 2026-10-12 + NBC Sports 2025-11-13 + FOX Sports 2026-02-05
- Rule text: "If the team that possesses the ball first scores a field goal on its initial possession, the other team (the second team) shall have the opportunity to possess the ball. (1) If the second team scores a touchdown on its possession, it is the winner. (2) If the second team scores a field goal on its possession, the team next scoring by any method shall be the winner. (3) If the second team does not score on its possession, the game is over, and the first team is the winner."
- NFL.com A.R. 16.6 (2012-era but still valid principle): "Team A kicks a field goal on the opening possession of overtime. Ruling: Three points for Team A, but the game is not over. Team A must kick off to Team B. If Team B scores a touchdown or does not score the game is over. If Team B scores a field goal the game continues under sudden death."
- ESPN 2010 rule (still describes the FG case): "after a first-possession field goal, the opposing team would also get an opportunity to possess the ball."
- If both teams have possessed and score remains tied → next score wins by any method.
- If still tied after 10 minutes → tie.

Playoffs (both-possess rule since 2022, 15-minute periods, play to winner):
- Same field-goal rule applies (both teams guaranteed possession regardless of first score).
- Each 15-minute period: if tied after both possess, next score wins; if still tied, another OT period (two-minute warning in 2nd/4th OT, coin toss after 4th OT).
- Defensive score or safety on opening possession ends game immediately (kicking team wins).
