# Should the engine go play-level? — my answer

Answering the prompt at `_agents/muse/deliveries/prompts-2026-11-05/council-prompt-2026-11-10-play-level.md`, informed by Z.ai's Plays Ledger answer (`_agents/vl/deliveries/z-play-by-play-analysis/_agents/zai/deliveries/ZAI-PLAY-LEVEL-ANSWER-2026-11-11.md`, council/vl `9ede94bf`) and MUSE's review of it (`inbox/20261111-0008-muse-P-341-zai-play-level-review.md`, VERDICT: improved). My independent re-verification of the worked numbers is in `inbox/20261111-0018-inkling-P-341-zai-review.md`.

**Verdict up front.** Yes — the Owner should let the engine *see* play level. No — the engine should not *become* a play-level simulator. The Plays Ledger (drives authoritative, plays staged inside each drive) is the right shape, and I recommend it. But the honest reason is not "it solves the derivation headaches" — it solves the subset that comes from *not measuring*, and it relocates the rest onto a new surface where it can be seen and argued about. The decision that matters is not drive vs. view vs. rebuild; it is whether the new layer is built with an honesty gate (measured against real football) or becomes a plausible-looking fiction. Everything below is aimed at that.

## 1. The Owner's question, answered directly

**Is the confusion a bigger headache than letting the engine watch its plays?** Mostly yes — with one honest split. The headaches that come from *not measuring* (FB/socialism, roles with no slot, gravity) are bigger than the machinery that ends them; that machinery is small and mostly already exists. The headaches that come from *worth* (C>OT) are real and no engine removes them; at play level they become visible, debatable rows instead of invisible constants. (Mirror of Z.ai's §0 and Z.ai is right on this split.)

**Would play level eliminate the headaches or move them?** Move them — onto better ground. C>OT survives as a value-layer question (ability 6.68 → tier 6; guard → 12; Owner 12 — VERIFIED by my run). The FB family is fixed by *measuring participation* instead of deriving it. The translation problem is not eliminated; it is upgraded from "fit my English into your constants" to "check my football against your mechanisms." That is an upgrade, not a victory, and it is the most the Owner should expect from any of this.

**Is the Owner right that we would not start from zero?** Yes. INFERRED estimate (~70% transfers INTACT, as Z.ai says — label it an estimate, not a conclusion): matchup model, drive generator, box score + purity contract, lineup optimizer + Hungarian + WINS_PER_UNIT, slot table, FLEX presentation, 99 rules as guide, duty vocabulary (17 duties), measurement culture (seeded seasons, LOO). Rebuilds: personnel model, attribution model, play sequencer (Z.ai's §5, size order). The calibrated numbers carry as *priors*, not as truths.

**Is the middle path (view/ Ledger) enough?** Enough for the FB family and Step 1's scope — yes. Enough for the whole vision — only if Step 4's measured-value loop actually ships, because the guard-as-filed with raw slot weights overshoots C>OG (max(ability 5.13 → 6 = Owner, guard 12.5 → 12 ≠ Owner) — VERIFIED). The design's "no misses" property is loaned from flexunify's measured values; without that loop it is not real. The view is not a compromise between two extremes; it is the shape whose failure modes stay contained (a bad staged play cannot break the score). That containment is worth a lot: it means the ledger can be wrong out loud.

## 2. The six interactions

**1. Is the Plays Ledger a third shape, or the play-view with a better name?** It is the view with a better name — and the name is load-bearing, not decorative. MUSE's "play-level view" and Z.ai's Ledger are the same architecture (drives authoritative, plays generated inside them). Z.ai adds three real things: the box-score existence proof (same artifact discipline: generated, additive, pure), the exposure-weighted guard with worked numbers, and a five-step path whose first step needs no sequencer. Those additions are not nothing, but they are not a new shape either. I would keep the name precisely because "play-level" frightens people into the simulator fear (Q: bottom-up rebuild); "ledger" tells the truth — an audit trail, not a second physics. Council should stop searching for a third shape; the interesting work is downstream of this one.

**2. Does the exposure-weighted guard survive the corrected numbers?** Conditionally, yes — and the corrections *strengthen* it. VERIFIED (re-ran Glyph's ZAI-D1 re-implementation with the Owner's three score changes; details in my review): FB>TE 4.74 (tier 6 = Owner), OLB>FS 11.74 (tier 12 = Owner), OLB>ILB 6.84 (6 = Owner), OLB>SS 8.12 (6 = Owner; binding gap ZCOV, not DEEP as Z.ai wrote), TE>FS 13.64 (12 = Owner), FB>RB 3.30 (tier 2), C>OG 5.13 (tier 6 = Owner), C>DT 9.12 (tier 12). Two caveats stand:
- **FB>RB is E-sensitive inside Z.ai's own band.** E·G ∈ [2.7, 4.5] straddles the tier cut at 4.0; the tier flips at E ≈ 0.222. The single "tier 2" verdict holds only for realistic FB shares (INFERRED: NFL FBs ~15–22% of offensive snaps when they play — to be *measured* at Step 1, not assumed). If the engine lands E ≈ 0.22+, FB>RB predicts 6 vs Owner 2, a 4-point gap — still inside the Owner's ±5 acceptance, but it must be reported with a sensitivity note, not as a point verdict.
- **C>OG is the one row the guard-as-filed breaks.** Ability 5.13 → tier 6 = Owner 6 exactly; adding E×guard with raw slot weights (E≈1) yields max(5.13, 12.5) = 12.5 → 12 ≠ Owner. This dies only when the measured-value loop shrinks the C→OG gap (snapping gives C real value OG lacks). Z.ai says this in one prose cell; the table does not show that it *reverses* an ability-only exact match. The five-step path must therefore pay the measured-value dependency (Step 4) before anyone claims "no unexplained rows."
The full guard picture with corrected abilities: 10 of 11 rows exact with raw stand-in, one (C>OG) repaired by measurement, one (FB>RB) within ±5 on E-stability. The guard is the right idea; the demo was filed prematurely.

**3. Does the Ledger solve the English-philosophy problem, or move it?** It moves it — onto terrain the Owner can state as fact. The personnel model ("which packages exist, who lines up where") is coach English, and it is falsifiable against real snap data. The attribution model ("who blocked whom, who doubled whom") is the dangerous one: nobody can state per-play blocking assignments for a whole season, so attribution *must* be generated by an authored grammar (edge-to-tackle transfer on pass rush, double teams on WR1-vs-WR4, etc.). That grammar is philosophy wearing a hard hat — it will decide what "double-team events" look like before anyone measures them. The honest framing: philosophy is not eliminated; it is **concentrated into two auditable artifacts** (packages and attribution rules) whose consequences are measured outputs, instead of diffused invisibly across fitted constants. Whether that is acceptable depends on enforcement: we must hold both artifacts to the nflverse ground truth (§4) or they will drift into fiction. Where philosophy *legitimately* remains is the value layer (what a center's absence is worth) — and that stays in the lab, as the Owner's argument, which is where he wanted it. Net: moved, not solved — but moved from unfalsifiable to arguable, which is the actual product goal.

**4. Is Step 1 shippable and sufficient?** Shippable — yes, INFERRED. It is the box score's contract with one more index (drives × plays × personnel → per-slot occupancy). The matchup model already treats personnel as first-class (`matchup.ts`: `'11'|'12'|'13'|'21'|'22'` in `OFF_OPTS`, `applyOffPlan`), so the occupancy table is authorable from what exists; v0 can use one package per drive (the drive's plan) and still yields real per-slot exposure. Package-per-drive is coarse (an FB sees 100% of run-heavy-short-yardage drives and 0% otherwise) — that is *the correct granularity for the FB problem*, and it is exactly the measure the Owner said cannot be intuited by formula. Sufficient? For the FB family alone, yes: with ability 3.30 (tier 2) and a measured E anywhere in the realistic band, FB>RB lands at tier 2 = Owner 2 (or within ±5 at the band edge). For C>OT, new roles, and gravity — no, Steps 2–5 and the measured-value loop are required. And the gate I would add before anything else is built: **validate the ledger against nflverse before Step 2** — the `dl5-snaps.py` pattern (VERIFIED: it ingests nflverse snap counts, PFR source, 2023–2025 team-games; note it only reads `defense_pct` — the claim that the same files carry *offensive* snap shares for FB/TE/WR is plausible but UNVERIFIED by me: I did not download the data). Four numbers, one afternoon: FB share of offensive snaps (target band ~15–25%, INFERRED); DIME < NICKEL < CB3; WR4/TE3 shares; DL5 vs the fifth-rank lineman distribution the lab already computes. If any is wildly off, fix the package shares before any downstream math exists. This gate is also the only thing standing between "we built a ledger" and "we built a confident fiction."

**5. Two loops, or one instrument?** Two — with one shared contract. The Ledger measures *exposure and attribution* (observational); it cannot answer "what would have happened with Player X at safety" — staged plays have no causal fork. The lab measures *worth* (perturb-and-resimulate; the `slotwins20` methodology; `WINS_PER_UNIT`); it cannot say who was on the field per play. Merging them is the full rebuild — the rejected shape. Merging costs you either causation (ledger-only can't price marginal worth) or contained failure (a rebuild can break the game in ways a view cannot). What the split costs: two instruments that can silently disagree — e.g., the lab prices a slot without snap exposure while the ledger measures exposure without value. The fix is contractual, not architectural: **one per-play event schema, three consumers** — the lab (marginal value), flex (prices), the advanced-stats section (the Owner's Q8 gravity). Share vocabulary (roles, duties, event types), not computation. If the Architect builds this, that schema is the one document worth getting reviewed before any code.

**6. What would Claude's answer have to say to change this?** I want Claude to argue; I also want the council to be honest that "causally consistent" is not an argument for rebuilding. The evidence that would settle rebuild-vs-view, on either side:
- A backtest showing the rebuilt play model reproduces the drive engine's *verified* outputs at least as well (drive endings, score distribution, seasons-to-SB, ~11.5 drives/team — the current calibration's realism is the asset; Z.ai's "the drive totals are the asset" is correct). Without it, rebuild is a bet, not a plan.
- A re-fit ledger: which calibrated constants (slot values, matchup polynomial, WINS_PER_UNIT usage) become stale under a rebuilt engine, and what re-measuring them costs.
- The determinism and error-containment story: the view cannot break the game; can the rebuild, provably, not?
- A smaller hand-translation surface than the Ledger's personnel+attribution models, measured in duty/role counts, not in adjectives.
If Claude's answer argues for the rebuild on those terms, the council should read it seriously. If it argues "bottom-up is more true to the football," that is aesthetics, and the Owner's Q3 already ruled: beautiful and portable wins — the Ledger is both. What would make me *prefer* the rebuild: proof that the view cannot deliver measurement fidelity (staged plays produce nonsense personnel patterns — 4-WR sets on 3rd-and-1 — that miscount attribution), or that drive totals are wrong in ways no staged view can audit. Neither is shown today.

## 3. The deeper question — can the engine generate the math?

Honestly? **No engine eliminates philosophy as input; the right question is whether philosophy becomes falsifiable.** Z.ai's two loops are the correct answer, and I would say it more plainly: the engine can generate *facts* (exposure, attribution, gravity — from the ledger) and *worth* (marginal win cost of a move — from the lab). A flex price is both multiplied together and gated by adjustable intent: `max(measured ability, E × measured guard)`, with the Owner's dial (flexunify's p-norm, Q7's adjustable ratio) left in place. What the Owner would still supply, in English, is the football — packages, assignments, what each job demands — and, legitimately, the verdicts ("done is a feeling that can be adjusted"). What he would stop supplying is *constants nobody can check*: no gates (his ruling), no floor (his ruling), no named exceptions (his ruling), no hand-fitted 18→2. "Beautiful and portable" (Q3) is served because the port is: re-author the football facts per sport, keep the machinery — the shape `max(ability, E × guard)` is sport-agnostic, and only the packages/duties re-translate. The 99 rules stop being the target and become what they always wanted to be: the Owner's reality contract, checked after the formula speaks, not before it is allowed to.

And the gravitational pull of the whole direction (Owner Q8): double-team events exist only at play level. The ledger is the first thing that can *formally measure* gravity — for offense (WR1 vs WR4 attention) and defense (rim protector / island corner, by analogy). That is the Owner's own most-repeated Q8 demand, and it is a user-visible feature (advanced stats), not just derivation plumbing.

## 4. The path — endorsed with four changes

Z.ai's five steps are the right order (snap ledger → sequencer → personnel/attribution → measured derivation → gravity ledger). I would change the emphasis and add gates:

1. **Ship the consumer before the payoff (show first, price second).** Wire Step 1's output into the advanced-stats / P-036 home early (usage, snap counts, "why you did well/poorly"). It pays for the layer with user-visible value, gives the Owner something to feel before Step 4, and de-risks the "is the ledger truthful?" question in public. Z.ai's own coda says this; I would make it part of the order, not an aside.
2. **Step 1 gate: nflverse validation before Step 2** (the four numbers in §2-4 of this filing). Until the FB/DIME/NICKEL/DL5 shares are compared against real football, every downstream measurement is INFERRED at best.
3. **Step 4 measure, don't promise:** report mean error + Spearman (the post-QA metrics), the center diagnostic (P-339: do centers stay at center, seeded drafts), the FB family (lands ≤ tier 6, and where the flip sits), LOO stability. The 99 remain the guide, never the target. And report E-sensitive rows with a sensitivity band, not a point price.
4. **C>OG first among the "escape" tests.** It is the row that proves the measured-value loop is load-bearing (ability exact, raw guard breaks it). If the loop cannot pull the C→OG gap under the owner tier cut, the whole exposure design needs to say so out loud, in the Owner's words: "this one is wrong, here's the dial." Done publicly, that is a feature; hidden, it is a failure mode.

Fail-fast checkpoints: after Step 1, wildly off real shares → fix package tables first (cheap). After Step 4, FB family fixed but center diagnostic fails → the value side is wrong, and we will know *which* half to argue (the split is the dividend). Keep those two gates explicit in the plan.

## 5. Sharp edges (so the Architect does not learn them the hard way)

- **Package-per-drive is coarse.** It under-measures FB in pass-down micro-usage (screens, flats). v0 is honest if labelled that way; do not let it pretend to be a play-by-play model before Step 2 exists.
- **Attribution is the philosophical sink.** Every attribution rule is a small authored theory of football. Keep them few, named, and event-falsifiable; if a rule cannot be checked against real double-team rates, it should not ship.
- **The lab and the ledger can drift.** They share vocabulary by contract; nobody reconciles them by default. Assign that reconciliation to a gate (LOO + ledger-agreement checks), not to good intentions.
- **The mathlab flex.json is already a live example of silent drift:** council/muse's copy is 97 rules and lacks OLB>FS; council/muse lacks nothing else but both the architect flex.json (99 rules) and `flex.ts` carry the 10 Nov changes (TE>FS → 12, OLB>FS new at 12). VERIFIED: I read both copies and `flex.ts`. This is exactly the failure mode the ledger's conservation discipline (parts sum to totals) exists to prevent — hold the ledger to the same standard on day one.
- **Determinism:** the ledger must be deterministic under a seed (Z.ai's Step 2 measurable: "same seed → same ledger"). Non-deterministic event counts would poison every "measured" claim downstream.
- **Do not let the ledger leak into the product's physics.** It is an audit trail and a measurement layer. If a user-visible outcome can disagree with the box score because of it, it has crossed the line.

## 6. One paragraph, in the Owner's terms

You asked whether deriving formulas from drive data plus your English is a bigger headache than letting the engine watch its own plays and do the math itself. Mostly yes — and the reason is plain: your principles keep breaking because the engine counts yards and points but never counts who was on the field and what they did, so each principle gets hand-translated into constants and re-fitted every round. If the engine, inside each drive it already creates, also writes down the plays that make up the drive — who lined up, who blocked, who doubled the wideout — your principles become measurements: the fullback's price falls out of how often he actually plays, a new role's price falls out of what it does on its plays, and the value side stays where it already lives, in the win math, now measured instead of guessed. You would still tell us the football — which packages exist, what each job demands (that part never emerges; you have said so) — but you would be stating it like a coach, not a spreadsheet, and it would be checkable. We would not start from zero, and we should not pretend to: the drive engine stays the truth, the box score already proves the parts can sum, and the calibrated numbers carry over as priors. Build the smallest piece first — snap counts by slot — and check it against real football before anything else. Then the fullback problem ends, the center row becomes an honest market row, and gravity finally exists for the advanced-stats section you asked for.
