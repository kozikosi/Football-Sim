**GLM F3 — softCap claim stated backwards — VERIFIED by my own code read.** `matchup.ts:87–91` (architect tip): `softCap` squashes log-rates into tight bands (rushLog −1.3..0.33, passLog −1.0..0.29, toLog ±0.7, skLog −0.8..0.4). The comment at line 84 states the purpose: "so a better defender is always worth something, even against an elite offense." Softmax saturation guarantees P ∈ [0,1] — which the log-linear family already had — and its saturation limit is P→0/1, i.e. an unbounded log-odds response, the opposite of a realism bound. GLM is right: the claim conflates two properties. His fix (a Phase-2 saturation-audit property gate + a pre-agreed fitted-squash fallback) is sound. (DS's D10 makes the same point from the other side — the behaviour returns as softmax saturation — and the two findings are consistent, not contradictory: the mechanism changes, the flattening behaviour does not.)

**GLM F4 — derivative is noise-free, not exact — VERIFIED.** The toy computes the derivative as a warm-started central finite difference (±2 gap units, lines 138–142). It is noise-free (solver tolerance 1e-9) but not mathematically exact — truncation error O(EPS²). GLM's fix (reserve "exact" for the solver's noise-free property; state the ~10⁴-state scope and the h policy) is correct.

His fix (the gap distribution is generated by the same lineup code the simulator runs, re-derived whenever the flex table changes) follows from the design's own requirement 3 ("Every constant has a named source"). Sound.

**DS D2 — the Phase 1 margin-pmf gate cannot pass at lambda = 0 — VERIFIED by reasoning.** §8's totals bar includes "margin pmf passes a goodness-of-fit test on held-out seasons" and Phase 1 is all lambda = 0 (neutral rosters). With lambda = 0 every team is identical, so the simulated margin pmf contains only within-game variance; the real pmf also contains the between-team quality component. A GoF test at 272 games × seasons will reject by construction. DS's fix (Phase 1 gates mean-level totals + the scoring grammar — tie rate, exact-3 and exact-7 margin spikes; the unconditional margin pmf GoF moves to Phase 2, on the equal-team conditional) is sound. This is the same class of finding as GLM's F2 and Inkling's Correction 1, and it is the most operationally important of the three: as written, the Phase 1 gate fails on a true baseline and would be read as "the engine does not work."

**DS D3 — lambda and theta are not separately identified — VERIFIED by algebra.** The formula is softmax_k[a + Σ_c λ_c·θ_{ν,k,c}·g_c]. For any channel c, (λ_c, θ) → (αλ_c, θ/α) leaves every probability unchanged. Only the product is identified from data. DS's fix (freeze θ at its prior while λ is estimated; normalise Σ_k θ = 1 per node/channel if θ is ever re-estimated; report λ as conditional on θ) is correct. This is a real defect in the design's parameter accounting, not a nit.

**DS D1 — the solver prices expected points; the flex table is in wins — VERIFIED as a gap.** The toy's state is (down, distance, yard line) with no score and no clock, and its value is net points to the next score. Every number the flex table is judged by is in wins (slotWins20, the 2/6/12 tiers). The EP → win-probability conversion (needs score/clock state or a margin model) and the per-game → per-season conversion are both missing and unstated. DS's fix (compute a flex price as a finite difference — two solves on the two lineups — and keep the derivative only as a linearisation check) is sound. (Glyph's review also notes the design flags "pricing on expected points ignores late-game policy" in §12, but DS is right that the gap is larger than late-game policy: EP is not wins.)

**DS D4 — dispersion matching overstates lambda by 23–75% — REPORTED, not independently re-run.** DS's synthetic (32-team, known λ = 1, Var(g) = 1, 20,000 leagues) shows dispersion matching at +23% (33% residual share), +43% (50%), +75% (67%), while regression Cov/Var is unbiased (1.002–1.003). I did not re-run his `p342-ident.mjs` in this session. The mechanism he names is sound — analytic dispersion matching attributes residual variance (opponent, scheme, luck, other channels) to the ratings — and the direction of the bias (overstates λ, overvalues stars) follows. His fix (estimate λ by regression Cov(y,g)/Var(g), or use dispersion matching in its simulated form) is correct in principle. This is the single most consequential finding in either review if it holds: a λ too large by 23–75% makes the MEASURED curve too steep and the flex prices too big. It needs one more seat to re-run `p342-ident.mjs` and confirm the numbers before the design is amended on its strength — but the design's estimator wording should be treated as suspect until then.
