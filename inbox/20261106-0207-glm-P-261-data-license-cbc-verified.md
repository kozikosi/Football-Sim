## CBC citation: my 5 Nov memory quote was wrong; the citation is right

I fetched the opinion itself on 6 Nov 2026 (OpenJurist, public domain, full text, VERIFIED):
**C.B.C. Distribution & Marketing, Inc. v. Major League Baseball Advanced Media, L.P.**, 505 F.3d 818
(8th Cir. 2007), docket nos. 06-3357, 06-3358, filed 16 Oct 2007 (rehearing denied 26 Nov 2007).
Source: https://openjurist.org/505/f3d/818/cbc-distribution-and-marketing-inc-v-major-league-baseball-advanced-media

- **Case name, citation, docket, outcome: all correct** in my 5 Nov answer (REPORTED there, now VERIFIED).
- **The reasoning I attributed from memory was wrong in direction and is retracted.** My 5 Nov text said the
  court found name+stat facts "'not copyrightable … federal copyright law does not pre-empt the players'
  claimed right of publicity'". Not what the opinion says: ¶10 — the district court *rejected* CBC's
  copyright-preemption argument; ¶24 — the Eighth Circuit *declined to reach* it ("we need not reach CBC's
  alternative argument that federal copyright law preempts the players' state law rights of publicity").
  Citing this case as a "facts are not copyrightable" holding misreads it.
- **What the court actually held** (two independent grounds, both needed for the "unlicensed use is lawful"
  result): (1) Missouri-law right-of-publicity elements were met (¶16-17) but the claim "must give way" to
  CBC's First Amendment rights — public-domain information + entertaining-but-protected speech + performance
  records as public-interest expression (¶20-23, citing Interactive Digital and Gionfriddo); (2) the 2002
  license's warranty that the MLBPA was "the sole and exclusive holder of all right, title and interest" in
  the names and statistics was breached (¶29), so the no-use/no-challenge provisions could not be enforced.
- **Limits now written into DATA-LICENSE.md layer D:** persuasive (not binding) outside the Eighth Circuit;
  Missouri-law-specific elements; a constitutional shield against state publicity claims, not a license.
- **Extra value from the fetch:** the opinion page lists a later, football-specific child holding —
  Dryer v. National Football League (D. Minn., 689 F.Supp.2d 1113) quotes CBC's records-as-public-interest
  passage; and CBS Interactive v. NFL Players Ass'n (259 F.R.D. 398, N.D. Minn.) is the football analogue
  (REPORTED from the OpenJurist citation list; not fetched — flagging as the natural next primary fetch if
  the Owner ever wants football-specific authority).
