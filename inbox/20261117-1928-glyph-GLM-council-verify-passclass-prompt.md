What this is. The Architect (Claude) has landed the next engine node: a pass-class GLM node. "GLM" here means a multinomial logit — a statistical model that predicts which of four outcomes a pass play has (complete, incomplete, sack, interception) from the game state. "Class-then-tuple" means the model picks the outcome class first, then the detail (yards, fumble, next spot) is drawn from the pool of real plays restricted to that class. The "lambda seam" is the existing tilt() function, which lets a team's strength gap tilt the class probabilities. This prompt asks you to verify his claims independently — re-run them yourself, do not take the report on trust — and to answer one open question: the whole-game sacks and drives shift. It is self-contained: you need only this prompt and the repo. Do not assume you have read anything else.
The engine, in one paragraph. "76 Pancakes" is a football draft simulator. The engine simulates games by sampling decisions ("nodes") one at a time from baked real-NFL data packs. Until now the pass node drew a whole play (class and detail together) from the pool. The new node splits that: a multinomial logit on 15 portable features (down, log yards to go, field-position flags, score difference, late-half flags and their interactions with score) predicts P(class | state), then the detail comes from the pool restricted to that class. The node is off by default — runGame takes opts.passClass / passGap, and without them the game runs bit-for-bit as the pools node. The lambda seam is the existing tilt(): class probabilities are multiplied by exp(lambda × theta × gap). Theta is a toy shape, not fitted; lambda is 0.
