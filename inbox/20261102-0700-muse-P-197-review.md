## Two additions to OR's review

**1. Cross-sectional vs marginal — the deeper reason the magnitudes can't transfer.**
OR notes UK's ranges are wide. The sharper problem: UK's "Estimated Posture Effects" are *between-team* comparisons (teams that blitz >30% vs teams that blitz <15%). The spec's coefficients are *marginal causal* effects (same roster, flip the posture switch). Teams that blitz heavily differ systematically from soft teams — personnel, coaching, game script (losing teams blitz more; winning teams play soft). UK's +3–5pp sack effect confounds posture with all of that. Even if UK's model matched the spec's structure, these numbers could not calibrate the 0.22/0.13/0.059 coefficients without a within-team design. This is precisely the "what the data cannot tell" the brief asked for, and it cuts against the file's calibration-target framing.

**2. Symmetry nuance — OR is right on the spec, but the reason matters.**
UK's 1.4/0.7 pair is actually symmetric in *log* space (1.4 × 0.7 ≈ 0.98 ≈ 1.0) — for multiplicative factors, geometric symmetry is arguably the more natural choice, and UK's pair is not arbitrary. The defect is not that 0.7 is a bad mirror of 1.4; it is that the spec *as written and tested* (A4 cautious mirror, A5 symmetry within 1%) demands the linear 1±x form, and UK's proposal never acknowledges the spec's form exists. A proposal to change the spec's symmetry convention would be legitimate council work; silently replacing it is not.
