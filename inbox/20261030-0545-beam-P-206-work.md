## RULES IN FORCE FOR THE 2026 SEASON (quoted from sources)

### Regular season (2025 rule change, carried into 2026)
Source: USAToday "How NFL overtime works in 2026" (2026-10-12); ESPN "What are NFL overtime rules" (2026-10-12); NFL Operations "2025 Rules Changes" (https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/featured-rules) — Rule 16-1-3 modified overtime to allow both teams an opportunity to possess the ball regardless of the outcome of the first possession, subject to a 10-minute overtime period.
- One 10-minute period after a 3-minute intermission.
- Coin toss: visiting captain calls; winner chooses to receive or defer goal.
- Both teams guaranteed a possession (unless the kicking team scores a safety on the receiving team's first possession → kicking team wins).
- After each team has possessed the ball, the next score wins (sudden death).
- Each team gets 2 timeouts; all reviews initiated by the replay official (no coach challenges).
- Point-after try not attempted if the game ends on a touchdown.
- If still tied after 10 minutes → game recorded as a tie.

### Playoffs (postseason rules, in force since 2022)
Source: Sporting News "NFL playoffs overtime rules" (2026-01-22); USAToday (2026-01-17).
- 15-minute periods; play continues until a winner (no ties).
- Each team guaranteed a possession; if tied after one possession each, next score wins.
- Exception: safety or defensive touchdown on first possession ends the game immediately.
- Each team gets 3 timeouts per half (two OT periods); same timing rules as end of 2nd/4th quarter at end of 2nd/4th OT period.
- 2-minute intermission between OT periods; no halftime after 2nd period.
- If still tied after 4th OT period, another coin toss and play continues until a winner.
- Extra points not attempted if game ends on a TD.

### First-possession touchdown case
- Under pre-2022 regular-season rules, a first-possession TD by the receiving team ended the game immediately (no opponent possession). This no longer applies in 2026 (both teams possess).
- Safety by the kicking team on the receiving team's first possession → kicking team wins (still in force).

## REAL FREQUENCIES (sourced)

### Share of games reaching overtime
- 6.1% of games reach overtime (1999-2025, nflverse game log, 7,276 games). Source: nflanalytic.com "How Often Do NFL Games Go to Overtime?" (2026-06-20).
- 2017 season: 17 OT games = 5.6% of games (sportsdaynow, 2022-10-02).
- 2025-2026 (through week 7 of 2026): 23 regular-season OT games.

### Tie rate
- Pre-2017 (15-min OT): 2.3% of OT games ended in a tie (7 ties / ~300 OT games, 1999-2016).
- Post-2017 (10-min OT): 5.7% of OT games ended in a tie (8 ties / ~140 OT games, 2017-2025).
- 2025-present new both-possess rule: 8.7% of OT games tied (2 / 23 through week 7 2026). Source: Wikipedia "List of NFL tied games" (2026-10-13).
- 1974-2011: 3.4% of OT games tied (17/494). 2012-2016: 6.0% (5/83). Source: NBC Sports Philadelphia (2021-09-11).
- Overall: ties in <1% of all NFL games since 1974.

### First-possession score rate
- Receiving team won 56.8% of OT games 2017-2024 (AP News, 2025-02-26, Troy Vincent).
- Under old (pre-2012) sudden-death rules: receiving team won 53% of OT games (theScore, 2022-01-28, 163 games).
- First-possession TD by receiving team ended only 21.4% of OT games (35 first-possession TDs + 3 pick-6s out of 163 games, 2012-2021 rules). Source: theScore (2022-01-28).
- Playoff OT (11 games under current rules): receiving team won 10/11, with 7 first-possession TDs; avg 1.8 possessions per game. Source: theScore (2022-01-28).
- Markov chain (modified sudden death): Team A (first possession) wins 55.4%, Team B 43.3%, tie 1.3%; average OT length 7 min 37 sec. With 10-min OT predicted: Team A wins 54.1%, tie probability rises to 11.2%. Source: Journal of Sports Analytics (iospress), Markov chain analysis of NFL overtime rules.

### Average OT length
