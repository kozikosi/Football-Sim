# Open call: invent new engine tests (Owner-directed)

The Owner wants active task development around engine testing — not just executing the queue, but inventing new ways to stress the math. If you're online now, this is for you.

## The pattern that works

Take a football intuition, push it to an extreme the engine must handle, measure what happens:

- "A great MIKE should blunt a great run game" → found the softCap zeroes him out (P-220)
- "The punter should matter" → found four features modeled but never simulated (P-221)
- "Corners shouldn't out-tackle safeties vs the run" → found the weights inverted (P-219)
- "The right gameplan should matter" → found the dial is decorative (P-222)

Each one was a 40-line script against `matchupModel`/`simulateGame` (extract via `git archive origin/architect/engine-and-seats colors data tools`). Hours of work, not days.

## Seed ideas (unclaimed — take one or invent your own)

- **4th-down logic**: does the sim go for it / punt / kick at the right times and field positions? Chart the decision boundary.
- **Home-field calibration**: HOME_FIELD = 1.7. Simulate identical teams home/away — is the realized edge sane vs the NFL's ~55-57%?
- **Comeback/clock logic**: 2-minute drill, timeouts, kneel-downs — does late-game behave, or does anything explode?
- **Turnover luck**: are forced turnovers sticky (skill) or pure noise week to week? Split-half correlation test.
- **Blowout behavior**: 99 vs 60 was sane (31-7, 98.8%). What about 99 vs 70? Where does competitiveness break?
- **Special teams beyond K**: kickoffs, onside kicks, return TDs — what's modeled, what's phantom?
- **The `lean` term**: team-identity run/pass lean — any feedback loop where a roster's identity distorts its own matchup?
- **Tiebreakers**: construct tied records, verify seeding is deterministic and sane.

## How to propose

File `inbox/<UTC>-<seat>-test-<slug>.md` with the hypothesis, the method, and what you'd measure. If it pans out, it becomes a P-item; if the engine survives, that's a result too — "tested, holds" is valuable. Review others' proposals the same way you review findings: try to break the method.
