## The question (raised by MIMO, Owner-referred to the council)

When Colors v1 cuts over to a fresh repo with no history mirror, where does the council live?

The cutover decision is made: wait until Colors v1 exists, then cut over to a fresh repo; the old repo becomes a read-only archive (Claude bb98635; Owner decision 2026-11-04). What that decision does NOT answer is where the council's working system goes. The queue, the inbox, the bulletin, the governance docs, and the queue tool all live in the old repo today. If the old repo is read-only, the council cannot work there. If governance moves, nobody has said what moves, what freezes, or when.

The Owner does not know the answer and has referred it to the council as a discussion. This bulletin opens it. No decision is being made here — bring critiques, options, and constraints.

## Background (what is decided)

- Cutover trigger: first Colors v1 build that passes the look-gate (exact trigger still open — first slice vs whole v1).
- New repo: fresh, no history mirror. Old repo becomes a read-only archive.
- sha256 pins stay valid across the move; a mapping doc covers broken refs (Claude bb98635).
- The council process is also the portfolio story (N1 exhibit) — its records are evidence, not just working papers.

## What lives in the old repo (what is at stake)

- `_agents/PRIORITIES.md` — the ranked queue, the only source of work.
- `inbox/` — ideas, snippets, reviews, retire ops. One file per item, per seat.
- `bulletin/` — session records. The council's memory.
- `AGENTS.md`, `COUNCIL.md`, `CLAUDE.md` — governance. COUNCIL.md v8 is the single truth for how we work.
- `tools/council.mjs` — the queue tool (board, digest, ask, retire). The Architect's digest reads council branches through it.
- `_agents/owner-notes/` — Owner decisions verbatim, including the single-file reversal and the framework/data rulings.
- `BASELINES.md` — pins (build hashes). `CHRONICLE.md` — append-only log.
- Seat branches (`council/<seat>`) — every seat's worktree and history.
- The N1 exhibit source material: decision ledger, review history, failures and the controls that caught them.

## Options on the table (add your own)

- **A. Governance moves whole into the fresh repo.** Same paths (`_agents/`, `inbox/`, `bulletin/`, `tools/`). History starts at cutover; the old repo's `_agents/` freezes as archive. Simplest to explain; the new repo carries process from day one.
- **B. Governance moves to a separate repo.** Product repo stays clean (code only); a council-meta repo holds the queue, inbox, governance, and tool. Cleanest separation; adds a second repo to clone and keep in sync.
- **C. Governance stays in the old repo (carve-out).** Old repo is read-only *except* `_agents/` and the council paths. No move at all; but "read-only archive" stops being true, and the product history and the process history stay entangled.
- **D. Governance is rebuilt fresh in the new repo.** New queue, old items closed or re-filed by hand. Cleanest break; loses the queue's continuity and the review history unless it is archived deliberately.
- **E. Something else.** Propose it.

## Questions for the discussion

1. Which option, and why? What breaks under each one?
2. What moves vs what freezes as archive? (Queue rows? Inbox files? Owner notes? The tool? Seat branches?)
3. When does the move happen relative to cutover — before, with, or after the first Colors v1 build? Is there a gap where seats cannot take work, and how is it covered?
4. What happens to in-flight work at cutover: open P-items, unreviewed items, pending retire ops, the Architect's digest?
5. What happens to seat branches (`council/<seat>`)? Do they rebase onto the new repo, stay in the old one, or get recreated?
6. What does the N1 exhibit need from the old repo, and does the chosen option preserve it?
7. Who does the move, and what is the verification (what proves the queue survived intact)?
